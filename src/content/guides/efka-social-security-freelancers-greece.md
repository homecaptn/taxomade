When digital nomads and remote workers look at relocating to Greece, they are usually obsessed with the famous [Article 5C 50% Tax Break](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c). While halving your income tax is incredible, there is a second, equally massive financial benefit that most expats completely overlook until they move: **Greek Social Security (EFKA).**

If you are a freelancer coming from countries like Germany, France, or Spain, social security contributions can be crippling. In Germany, a successful freelancer can easily pay €800 to €1,000+ per month just for public health insurance and pension. 

In Greece, a newly registered freelancer pays approximately **€150 to €180 per month** for full public healthcare and pension coverage. Here is how the system works.

## How EFKA Works for Freelancers

EFKA (ΕΦΚΑ) is the unified social security fund in Greece. If you set up a Sole Proprietorship (ατομική επιχείρηση) to work as a freelancer or contractor, you are legally required to register with EFKA and pay monthly contributions.

Unlike in many other European countries, your EFKA contributions in Greece **are not a percentage of your income.** Instead, Greece uses a tiered category system. You get to *choose* which category you want to belong to.

### The Standard Categories
Every year, you select an insurance category. The lowest standard category costs roughly €240 to €250 per month, while the highest category costs over €600 per month (higher categories result in a higher state pension upon retirement). Regardless of whether you earn €20,000 or €200,000 a year, your social security cost is fixed based on the category you chose.

## The "Starter Rate" for New Freelancers

Here is where the massive benefit comes in for expats moving to Greece: **The Special Category for New Professionals.**

To encourage entrepreneurship, the Greek government offers a heavily discounted EFKA rate for new businesses. For the **first 5 years** after registering your Sole Proprietorship, you are eligible for the "Starter Category" (Ειδική Κατηγορία).

As of recent rates, this category costs **approximately €150 to €180 per month** (rates adjust slightly each year for inflation, typically around +6%). 

This single, low monthly payment covers:
- **Public Healthcare coverage** (access to state hospitals and subsidized medications).
- **Pension contributions.**
- **Unemployment fund contributions.**

For five years, no matter how much your business grows, your social security costs remain capped at this incredibly low flat rate. 

## Remote Employees vs. Freelancers

It is important to note that this applies to *freelancers* (Sole Proprietors). What if you are a remote employee?

If you live in Greece but remain employed directly by your foreign employer (and you are an EU citizen), you generally **do not pay EFKA contributions at all.** Instead, you remain in your home country's social security system. You simply need to obtain an **AMKA** (Greek Social Security Number) and an **A1 Certificate** from your home country to access the Greek healthcare system locally. Your foreign employer handles your home-country social security deductions as usual.

## The Bottom Line

When calculating your net income in Greece, you must factor in both Income Tax and EFKA. Thanks to the 5-year starter rate, the social security burden in Greece is negligible compared to Northern Europe. 

Coupled with the 50% income tax break, a freelancer earning €80,000 a year in Greece will take home significantly more net profit than they would in almost any other Western European country.
