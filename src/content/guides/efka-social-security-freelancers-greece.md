When remote workers look at relocating to Greece, they usually focus on the [Article 5C 50% tax break](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) (available if you were not a Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years). There is a second financial benefit that most people overlook until they move: the cost of social security, **EFKA**, for new freelancers.

If you are a freelancer coming from Germany, France or Spain, social security is a large fixed cost. In Germany, a well-earning freelancer can pay €700 to €1,000 per month for statutory health and long-term-care insurance alone, before any pension saving.

In Greece, a newly registered freelancer pays about **€160 per month** (2026: €150.46 plus a €10 unemployment contribution for most freelancers) for public healthcare and pension coverage. Here is how the system works.

## The short answer

- **Regular EFKA, lowest class:** €250.77 per month in 2026. Fixed amount, not income-based.
- **New freelancers:** reduced class of €150.46 per month for the first five years, on application, usable once.
- **On top:** €10 per month unemployment contribution for most freelancers.
- **What you get:** public healthcare and pension credit.
- **Remote employees** of a foreign company: different rules, see below.

## How EFKA works for freelancers

EFKA (ΕΦΚΑ) is the unified social security fund in Greece. If you [set up a sole proprietorship](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece) (ατομική επιχείρηση) to work as a freelancer or contractor, you are required to register with EFKA and pay monthly contributions.

Unlike in many other European countries, your EFKA contributions in Greece **are not a percentage of your income**. Greece uses a tiered category system, and you choose the category you belong to.

### The standard categories

Every year, you select an insurance category. The lowest standard category costs €250.77 per month in 2026, the highest €675.87 (higher categories build a higher state pension). Whether you earn €20,000 or €200,000 a year, your social security cost is fixed by the category you chose. The amounts are adjusted for inflation every year ([EFKA circular 6/2026](https://www.forin.gr/articles/article/88941/efka-egk-6-2026): +2.5% for 2026).

## The starter category for new freelancers

To encourage new businesses, EFKA offers a reduced category for new professionals, the Ειδική Κατηγορία ("special category"). It applies for the **first five years of your insurance as self-employed**, counted from your first registration as self-employed in Greece, and can be used only once.

In 2026 this category costs **€150.46 per month**, plus the €10 unemployment contribution for most freelancers. You have to apply for it; it is not assigned automatically.

This single monthly payment (about €160 all-in) covers:

- **Public healthcare** (access to state hospitals and subsidised medication).
- **Pension contributions.**
- **The unemployment fund** (via the €10 contribution).

For five years, no matter how much your business grows, your social security cost remains at this flat rate, adjusted only for inflation each year.

## Remote employees vs. freelancers

The starter category applies to *freelancers* (sole proprietors). What if you are a remote employee?

If you live in Greece but stay on the payroll of a foreign employer, the answer depends on where you physically work. The EU default rule (Regulation 883/2004) is that you are insured where you work, and if that is your desk in Athens, that is Greece. Greece has not joined the EU framework agreement on cross-border telework, so a full-time remote employee living in Greece normally falls under EFKA, and the foreign employer has to register with EFKA or appoint the employee to handle the contributions on its behalf; many companies use an Employer of Record instead.

The exceptions are a formal posting (A1 certificate from your home country, normally capped at 24 months) or genuinely splitting your working time between Greece and your home country, with a substantial share at home. In those cases your home system keeps covering you, and an S1 form gives you access to Greek public healthcare. Employees from outside the EU (UK, US) fall under bilateral agreements or Greek rules directly.

This is one of the areas where guessing gets expensive. Have it checked before you move; the employment side of the tax regime is in our guide on [5C for employees and remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers).

## The bottom line

When calculating your net income in Greece, factor in both income tax and EFKA. Thanks to the five-year starter rate, the social security burden for new freelancers is low compared with Northern Europe.

Coupled with the 50% income tax break, a freelancer on €80,000 a year keeps noticeably more in Greece than in Germany, France or Spain. Run the numbers with our [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026), because advance tax and the presumptive minimum income still apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is EFKA for freelancers in Greece in 2026?

The lowest regular class is €250.77 per month. New freelancers can apply for €150.46 per month for their first five years. A €10 unemployment contribution comes on top for most freelancers. Contributions are fixed per class, not a percentage of income.

### Who qualifies for the reduced EFKA starter class?

Anyone insured with EFKA as self-employed for the first time, for the first five years of that insurance. You apply for it, and you can use it only once.

### What does EFKA cover?

Public healthcare, pension credit and, with the €10 contribution, the unemployment fund. The starter class gives full healthcare access from the first payment.

### Do remote employees of a foreign company pay EFKA in Greece?

Usually yes, if they work from Greece full-time. Exceptions are a formal posting with an A1 certificate and multi-state work with a substantial share in the home country.
