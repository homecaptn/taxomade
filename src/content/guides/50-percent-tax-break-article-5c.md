Greece wants to attract professionals who move their tax residency to the country, foreigners and returning Greeks alike. The centrepiece is **[Article 5C of the Greek Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/5Γ)**, a scheme that exempts 50% of your employment or business income from Greek income tax for seven years. Nationality doesn't matter; your tax history does.

Here is how the 50% tax break works, who qualifies, and how to claim it.

## The short answer

- **What you get:** income tax on only **50%** of your Greek employment or business income, for **7 tax years**.
- **Main condition:** not Greek tax resident in **5 of the previous 6 years**.
- **Qualifying activity:** a job with a Greek employer (or the Greek branch of a foreign company), or your own individual business in Greece.
- **Deadline:** start by 2 July → apply by 31 December that year; start later → by 31 December of the next year.
- **Not covered:** EFKA contributions, and Digital Nomad Visa holders.

## What the 5C tax break is

Under Article 5C, qualifying individuals who transfer their tax residency to Greece pay income tax on only **50% of their employment or business income**. In addition, the deemed annual living costs of your home and private car are not counted when Greece calculates presumptive income.

The incentive applies for **seven tax years** and cannot be extended. Social security contributions (EFKA) are not reduced. Because the remaining 50% is taxed on the progressive scale, the actual saving is often more than half of your normal tax.

There is no minimum income. Whether you earn €30,000 or €300,000, you can qualify; the higher your income, the more you save.

## Who qualifies

You must meet *all* of the following conditions:

1. **Previous non-resident status:** you were not a Greek tax resident in at least 5 of the 6 years before the move.
2. **Where you come from:** you transfer your tax residency from an EU or EEA member state, or from a country with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement on tax matters.
3. **Activity in Greece:** you take up employment with a Greek legal entity or the Greek permanent establishment of a foreign company, **or** you register an individual business (sole proprietorship, ατομική επιχείρηση) in Greece. Profits from a company such as an IKE are not covered: only salary from a Greek employer or income from your own sole proprietorship. Fees paid to a director or managing partner of a Greek company count as employment income ([AADE circular E.2224/2021](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2021-12/e_2224_2021ada.pdf)); dividends and company profits do not.
4. **Intent to stay:** you declare that you will remain in Greece for at least two years, counted from 1 January of the first year of the regime.

[Law 5222/2025](https://taxblock.gr/forologikos-odigos-template/katargisi-tis-par-6-tou-arthrou-5g-kfe-ti-allazei-me-ton-n-5222-2025/) removed the condition that a job had to be a "newly created position", effective 28 July 2025. And since [decision A.1096/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/52732/a-1096-2026) (spring 2026), people moving to work in the Greek public sector can also apply, including for applications filed from 28 July 2025.

## How it works for remote workers

If you are a remote worker employed by a foreign company (for example in the US or UK), simply moving to Greece and working from your laptop does **not** give you the 5C tax break.

Digital Nomad Visa holders are **not eligible**. The DNV does not allow work for Greek employers or Greek business activity, so its holders cannot meet the employment or business condition. See [5C vs Digital Nomad Visa](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa/).

For EU citizens, and non-EU citizens with a permit that allows self-employment, the usual route is:

1. Register as self-employed (sole proprietorship) in Greece.
2. Invoice your foreign employer, who becomes your client, through your Greek business.
3. Apply for 5C as a new Greek freelancer.

This route has one trap, covered in mistake 5 below. If you would rather stay an employee, the alternative is a job with a Greek company or the Greek branch of a foreign company. See [5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers/).

## Deadlines and how to apply

You cannot apply before you move. First you relocate, get your tax number (AFM), and register your employment or business. Then you, or your accountant, file the application digitally in myAADE.

The deadline depends on when your job or business starts ([AADE](https://www.aade.gr/psifiakes-ypiresies/eisodima/ypoboli-aitisis-ypagogis-se-eidiko-tropo-forologisis-arthro-5g-toy-kfe)):

- **Start on or before 2 July:** apply by **31 December of the same year**. The regime then covers that year.
- **Start after 2 July:** apply by **31 December of the following year**. The regime starts in that following year.

If you started by 2 July, a late application within the following year shifts the regime to that year; after that, the window is gone.

The procedure follows decision A.1087/2021 as rewritten by [A.1138/2025](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/51291/a-1138-2025). For employees, the system checks your employment automatically against the ERGANI register; if everything matches, the decision is immediate. Otherwise you upload documents, typically a tax residence certificate from your previous country, and the tax office decides within 60 days. If it asks for more documents, you have 30 days to supply them; if the application is rejected because they are missing, you can still submit them by 31 March of the following year (A.1138/2025). 5C can run alongside Article 5A or 5B if you meet the conditions of each ([AADE 5C FAQ](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2025-10/FAQS_5G_KFE.pdf)). Once approved, employers withhold tax on only the taxable half, and the exemption also appears in your annual return.

## The five most common mistakes

The rules are strict, but more forgiving than their reputation: a late application usually shifts your first year, and missing documents can often be supplied later. The expensive mistakes happen before you apply, in how and when you set up.

### 1. Planning the start date without the 2 July cut-off

The start date of your job or business decides your first 5C year. Register a sole proprietorship on 15 March and you can apply until 31 December for that same year. Register on 10 July and your first 5C year is the following one. Plan the start date with this in mind.

### 2. Proving non-residence with the wrong paperwork

A foreign passport proves nothing here. For each year that Greek tax records don't already show you as non-resident, you need proof from abroad: a certificate of tax residence from the foreign tax authority, or the residence form under the double tax treaty. If a country doesn't issue such certificates, your tax assessment or the return you filed there as a resident can be used instead. Order them early; some tax offices take weeks.

Treaty residence forms are accepted without an apostille; other foreign documents may need one, plus a Greek translation. If an application is rejected for missing documents, it is not over: submit them by 31 March of the following year and the rejection is revoked for the original year.

### 3. Setting up an IKE instead of a sole proprietorship

The business route covers an individual business only, not a company such as an IKE. A salary from a Greek company, including one you own, falls under the employment route instead, with its own conditions. If you want an IKE later, see [IKE setup and annual costs](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs/), but get the 5C structure right first.

### 4. Applying while on a Digital Nomad Visa

As explained above, the Digital Nomad Visa rules out the employment and business conditions, so the application fails. Non-EU citizens need a residence permit that allows Greek employment or self-employment before they register; EU citizens need no visa at all.

### 5. Billing your former employer without thinking it through

Invoicing your old employer through a new Greek sole proprietorship works, but if that employer stays your only client under the same working conditions, the arrangement can be challenged as disguised employment, in Greece or in your client's country. And the 50% applies to income tax only: EFKA contributions are paid in full, and VAT returns, myDATA invoicing and the annual return still apply.

## Keeping the break once you have it

The regime ends from the year you move your tax residence abroad, or if your Greek job or sole proprietorship stops for more than 12 months without a new one. You also commit to staying at least two years, counted from 1 January of your first 5C year.

## A 2026 addition: carried interest

Since 2026, [Law 5313/2026](https://www.ey.com/en_gr/technical/tax/tax-alerts/new-tax-provisions-on-individuals-transferring-tax-residency-in-greece-and-alternative-investment-funds) taxes carried interest at 5% instead of 15% for 5C employees of a Greek entity that services investment fund managers and spends at least €3 million a year in Greece. It runs for up to seven years. It is a niche rule for the fund industry; the core 50% exemption and its conditions did not change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who qualifies for the 50% tax break in Greece?

People who move their tax residence to Greece from an EU/EEA country or a country with a tax cooperation agreement, were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years, take up employment with a Greek employer (or the Greek branch of a foreign company) or start an individual business in Greece, and declare they will stay at least two years.

### What is the deadline to apply for Article 5C?

If your job or business starts on or before 2 July, apply by 31 December of the same year. If it starts after 2 July, apply by 31 December of the following year. If you started by 2 July and miss that year's deadline, you can still apply in the following year, but the regime then starts in that year.

### What documents prove I was not a Greek tax resident?

Only for the years Greek records don't already show you as non-resident: a certificate of tax residence from the foreign tax authority (or the residence form under the double tax treaty), otherwise your foreign tax assessment or return. Treaty forms are exempt from the apostille.

### What if my 5C application is rejected for missing documents?

You can submit the missing documents by 31 March of the following year; the tax office then revokes the rejection and issues a new decision for the original year.

### Can remote workers for foreign companies get 5C?

Not as employees of a foreign company without a Greek presence. The usual route is to register as self-employed in Greece and invoice the foreign company as a client, or to work for a Greek employer or the Greek branch of a foreign company.

### Can Digital Nomad Visa holders apply for 5C?

No. The Digital Nomad Visa does not allow work for Greek employers or Greek business activity, so its holders cannot meet the employment or business condition of 5C.

### Does Article 5C reduce EFKA social security contributions?

No. The 50% exemption applies to income tax only. EFKA contributions are paid in full.
