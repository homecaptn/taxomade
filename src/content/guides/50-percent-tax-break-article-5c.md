Greece wants to attract professionals who move their tax residency to the country, foreigners and returning Greeks alike. The centrepiece is **[Article 5C of the Greek Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/5Γ)**, a scheme that exempts 50% of your employment or business income from Greek income tax for up to seven years. Nationality doesn't matter; your tax history does.

Here is exactly how the 50% tax break works, who qualifies, and how to claim it.

## 1. What is the Article 5C Tax Break?

Under Article 5C, qualifying individuals who transfer their tax residency to Greece will only pay income tax on **50% of their Greek-sourced employment or freelance income**. In addition, the deemed annual living costs of your home and private car are not counted when Greece calculates presumptive income.

The incentive applies for up to **seven tax years**. Social security contributions (EFKA) are not reduced.

## 2. Who Qualifies? (The Conditions)

To successfully apply for the 50% tax break, you must meet *all* of the following conditions:

1. **Previous Non-Resident Status:** You must not have been a Greek tax resident for at least 5 of the previous 6 years prior to the transfer of your tax residency.
2. **Relocation Source:** You must be transferring your tax residency from an EU or EEA member state, or a country with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement on tax matters.
3. **Active Employment/Freelancing:** You must be taking up new employment in Greece (with a Greek legal entity or a Greek branch of a foreign company) OR starting a freelance/sole proprietorship business in Greece. 
4. **Intent to Stay:** You must declare your intention to remain in Greece for at least two years.

> [!TIP]
> **No Minimum Income Requirement:** Article 5C has no minimum income threshold. Whether you earn €30,000 or €300,000, you can qualify—and the higher your income, the more you save.

### The 2025 "New Job" Abolition
Law 5222/2025 removed the condition that a job had to be a "newly created position", effective 28 July 2025. In practice, this mattered mainly for employees, whose employers previously had to confirm that the position was new. Since 2026, people working in the Greek public sector can also apply.

## 3. How It Works for Remote Workers

If you are a remote worker employed by a foreign company (e.g., in the US or UK), simply moving to Greece and working from your laptop does **not** automatically grant you the 5C tax break.

> [!WARNING]
> **Digital Nomad Visa Incompatibility:** If you are a Non-EU citizen entering Greece on the Digital Nomad Visa (DNV), you are **not eligible** for the 5C tax break. The DNV does not allow work for Greek employers or Greek business activity, so DNV holders cannot meet the employment or business condition of 5C. See [5C vs Digital Nomad Visa](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa).

To qualify, you must integrate into the Greek tax system. For EU citizens (or Non-EU citizens on specific business/work visas), the best route is:
1. **Set up a Sole Proprietorship (Freelancer) in Greece.**
2. Bill your foreign employer (who essentially becomes your B2B client) through your Greek business entity.
3. Apply for the 5C scheme as a new Greek freelancer.

This lets you invoice clients abroad while paying Greek income tax on only half of that business income. One caution: if your former employer becomes your only client under the same working conditions, the arrangement can be challenged as disguised employment, in Greece or in your client's country. The alternative for employees is a job with a Greek company or the Greek branch of a foreign company. See [5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers).

## 4. Application Deadlines and Process

You cannot apply for this tax break *before* you move. 

First, you must relocate, obtain your tax number (AFM), and register your employment or business. Once established, you submit your application for the 5C regime to the relevant Greek Tax Authority.

The deadline depends on when your job or business starts ([AADE](https://www.aade.gr/psifiakes-ypiresies/eisodima/ypoboli-aitisis-ypagogis-se-eidiko-tropo-forologisis-arthro-5g-toy-kfe)):

- **Start on or before 2 July:** apply by **31 December of the same year**. The regime then covers that year.
- **Start after 2 July:** apply by **31 December of the following year**. The regime starts in that following year.

Applying later doesn't necessarily cost you the regime; it usually shifts the first year it applies to. The application is filed online in myAADE. Where the system can verify your data automatically, the decision comes quickly. Otherwise you upload documents and the tax office decides, normally within 60 days. For employees, the employer withholds tax on only the taxable half once you're approved; the exemption is also reflected in your annual return.

## Summary

The Article 5C tax break is one of the most generous incentives in Europe. Because the remaining 50% is taxed on a progressive scale, the tax saving is often more than half. It does require a correct setup: the right start date, a qualifying job or business in Greece, and an application in the right year.
