Your bank abroad wants proof that you now live in Greece for tax purposes. Your old country's pension office wants to stop withholding tax. A German broker wants a treaty form. In each case the answer is the same document: the Greek **tax residence certificate** (πιστοποιητικό φορολογικής κατοικίας, ΠΦΚ), issued online by AADE.

This guide covers the certificate itself. Whether you are a Greek tax resident in the first place is a separate question, covered in [tax residency in Greece and the 183-day rule](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days/).

## The short answer

- **What it is:** an official AADE document confirming you are a Greek tax resident under Article 4 of the Income Tax Code, for a specific year.
- **How to get it:** online only, in myAADE under *Registry & Communication → Tax Residence Certificate*, with your personal TAXISnet codes. Issued immediately, free.
- **Language:** Greek and English on the same document, with an electronic seal and QR code.
- **Which years:** the current year, plus past years for which you filed a Greek return as a resident. Never the following year.
- **Typical blocker:** the tax register still showing you as non-resident or with a foreign address.
- **Foreign forms:** AADE no longer stamps them. You send the foreign form together with the Greek certificate.

## What the certificate is (and what it is not)

The certificate confirms one thing: that Greece regards you as tax resident for a given year. Since [AADE decision A.1052/2023](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2023-05/a_1052fek.pdf) (ΦΕΚ Β' 2907/03.05.2023), it is issued only through an online application. Paper requests to a tax office have no legal effect, and local tax offices no longer issue the "confirmation" that used to come first.

There are two types:

- **For a double tax treaty (ΣΑΔΦΕ):** names the treaty country and, where relevant, the type of foreign income. You need one per country, per year, per income category.
- **For other purposes:** names no country. Used for banks, tenders and anything that just needs proof of Greek tax residence.

It is **not** your AFM printout, a registry extract or a residence permit. And it is not the document you need when *leaving* Greece: to transfer your residence abroad, AADE wants a certificate from your new country (see the [183-day guide](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days/)).

## Who can get one

Greek tax residents only: individuals, and companies or other entities through their legal representative. In practice that means:

- your registry entry in TAXISnet shows you as **resident in Greece** with a **Greek address**;
- your contact details (email, mobile, landline) in myAADE are up to date, or the application sends you to update them first;
- for past years, you have **filed the Greek return as a resident** for each year you request.

Nationality doesn't matter.

## How to apply online

### Step by step

1. Log in to [myAADE](https://aade.gr/en/tax-services/income/application/issuance-tax-residence-certificate-trc) with your personal TAXISnet codes and open **Μητρώο & Επικοινωνία → Πιστοποιητικό Φορολογικής Κατοικίας** (Registry & Communication → Tax Residence Certificate). The same service is linked from [gov.gr](https://www.gov.gr/en/org/aade/phorologia-politon/pistopoietiko-phorologikes-katoikias).
2. Choose the **purpose**: treaty application or other purposes.
3. Choose the **year**, and for treaty use the **country** and **income category**.
4. Check your name and address in Latin characters. You can adjust up to two characters per word to match your passport spelling; the AFM can't be changed.
5. Confirm the **sworn declaration** that you will file (or have filed) a Greek return for that year including all foreign income. It counts as a solemn declaration under Law 1599/1986, so it has to be true.
6. Download the PDF.

### Processing time and cost

The certificate is generated automatically on submission, free of charge. Before 2023, the manual process took three to five days ([AADE press release](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2023-05/d.t._03.05.2023.pdf)).

### Current year vs past years

- **Current year:** available as soon as the register shows you as resident, based on your declaration that you will file.
- **Past years:** AADE's [FAQ of December 2025](https://aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-01/FAQs_PFK_en.pdf) lists every year back to 2014. Under A.1052/2023, a past-year certificate requires that you filed that year's return as a Greek resident, and for treaty use you confirm the foreign income was included. AADE's older service page still says "current or previous year", so if the application doesn't offer an earlier year, contact AADE rather than assume it is available.
- **Next year:** not possible.

### Language, apostille and verification

The certificate is bilingual, Greek and English, so translation is only needed if the receiving authority demands it. Authenticity is checked by its QR code and unique issue number.

AADE says [most countries accept it without an apostille](https://aade.gr/en/tax-services/income/application/issuance-tax-residence-certificate-trc). If yours doesn't, issue the digital version first, then ask AADE's International Tax Relations Directorate (ΔΔΦΟΣ, Peiraios 180, Tavros) for a hand-signed paper replacement, either in person, by post, or through *My Requests* (Τα Αιτήματά μου) in myAADE with the digital certificate attached. That paper version can then be apostilled.

## What you will use it for

### Proving residence to your former country

Your old tax office may keep treating you as resident until you show where you are now taxed. A German Finanzamt, for example, may ask for it when you claim that your income after the move belongs to Greece. Our guides on the [Germany–Greece tax treaty](/guides/germany-greece-double-tax-treaty/) and the [year of the move](/guides/germany-greece-double-tax-year-of-move/) show where it fits.

### Treaty relief on dividends, interest and pensions

Foreign payers withhold tax at their domestic rate unless you prove treaty residence elsewhere. With a treaty certificate, you can claim the reduced rate up front or a refund afterwards. Typical cases:

- **Pensions:** where the treaty gives Greece the right to tax, the paying fund or foreign tax office typically wants the certificate before it stops withholding or exempts the pension. See [Greek tax on foreign pensions](/guides/greek-pension-tax-foreign-retirees/).
- **Dividends:** for example, German withholding of 26.375% can be reduced to the 25% treaty rate through a refund claim.

AADE no longer fills in or stamps foreign claim forms. You complete the foreign form yourself and attach the Greek certificate. AADE states that treaty partners have been informed and accept the digital version, but the receiving authority has the final say, so check its requirements before you file.

### Banks and CRS

Foreign banks and brokers must classify you under the OECD's Common Reporting Standard. When you update your address to Greece, they usually ask for a self-certification, and some ask for official proof. The "other purposes" certificate covers that.

### Special tax regimes: 5B and 5C

Under the 7% regime for pensioners ([Article 5B](/guides/retiring-to-greece-7-percent-flat-tax/)) or the 50% income tax exemption ([Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/)), you become Greek resident through the regime application itself. Once that is approved and the register is updated, the Greek certificate is how you prove the new status abroad, for instance to stop pension withholding. The 5C application itself needs proof from *abroad* that you weren't Greek resident before, not a Greek certificate.

## Common problems

### The register still says "non-resident"

Many people get their AFM as non-residents before moving, often through a representative. That status doesn't change automatically when you arrive. Until it does, the application won't issue a certificate. The fix is a residence change through *My Requests → Registry → Changes of personal information* (Μεταβολές στοιχείων φυσικού προσώπου), with form Δ210 and the supporting documents AADE asks for. Applicants under 5A, 5B or 5C are excluded from this registry route; their status is handled through the regime application ([AADE FAQ for non-residents](https://aade.gr/sites/default/files/2025-11/FAQs_omogeneis_gr_0.pdf)).

### Address still abroad in TAXISnet

A foreign address in your registry entry, or an outdated phone or email, blocks the application. Update it first; the certificate prints whatever address the register holds.

### First-year movers who haven't filed yet

You can get a certificate for the current year once the register is updated. What you can't get is a certificate for your arrival year before you've filed that year's [E1 return](/guides/greek-tax-return-e1-foreigners/), normally due by mid-July of the following year. If you arrived in autumn and need proof for that year in spring, plan for the wait or file early.

### Is there a manual route?

For living taxpayers, essentially no: requests filed outside the application are treated as not submitted. AADE only issues certificates by hand for deceased persons, plus the signed paper versions for apostilles. If the application refuses you, the answer is to fix the underlying registry or filing gap, not to visit the tax office.

### Revocation

If AADE later finds that your residence changed, the International Tax Relations Directorate can revoke the certificate. Don't apply for a year in which you weren't actually resident.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get a tax residency certificate in Greece?

Online only, through AADE's myAADE portal under Registry & Communication (Μητρώο & Επικοινωνία), Tax Residence Certificate, or via gov.gr, with your personal TAXISnet codes. You choose the year, the purpose and, for treaty use, the country. The certificate is issued immediately and free of charge.

### Is the Greek tax residence certificate issued in English?

Yes. Every certificate is bilingual, Greek and English, so it usually needs no translation. It carries a unique issue number, AADE's electronic seal and a QR code for verification.

### Can I get a Greek tax residency certificate in my first year after moving?

For the current year, yes, if the tax register already shows you as a Greek resident with a Greek address. You declare that you will file a Greek return for that year, including foreign income. A certificate for a past year requires the Greek return for that year to be filed first.

### Does the Greek tax residence certificate need an apostille?

Most countries accept the digital certificate without one. If an authority insists on an apostille or legalisation, you first issue the digital certificate and then ask AADE's International Tax Relations Directorate for a hand-signed paper version, which can be apostilled.

### Will AADE stamp my foreign tax form, such as a German residence confirmation?

No. AADE no longer completes or stamps other countries' forms. You fill in the foreign form yourself and submit it together with the digital Greek certificate, which AADE says treaty partners have been informed about and accept.

### How many years back can I get a Greek tax residence certificate?

AADE's FAQ of December 2025 lists the current year and past years back to 2014, although AADE's older service page still mentions only the current or previous year. Future years are not possible. For any past year, you need a Greek tax return for that year filed as a resident.
