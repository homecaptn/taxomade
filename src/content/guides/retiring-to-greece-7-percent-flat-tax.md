Foreign pensioners who move to Greece can opt to pay a flat **7% on all their foreign income, for 15 years**. The rules changed in 2026: the application deadline is now **31 October** of the tax year, not 31 March as older guides still say. This guide covers who qualifies, how to apply and pay, the treaty traps and the rest of the move: residence, healthcare and the first-year admin. Whether the 7% is actually cheaper than the normal scale for your pension is worked through in our [Greek pension tax guide](/guides/greek-pension-tax-foreign-retirees/).

## The short answer

- **7% flat on all foreign-source income** (pensions, but also foreign rent, dividends and interest) for **15 tax years**, under Article 5B of the Income Tax Code.
- **Conditions:** a pension from abroad, **not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years**, and a move from a country with a tax cooperation agreement with Greece (EU states and many treaty partners; check yours).
- **New deadline:** apply by **31 October** of the year you want the regime for; documents by 30 November; pay by the last working day of **December**, in one go.
- **Not automatic for couples:** each spouse qualifies and applies separately.
- **Government pensions** (UK civil service, German Beamte) usually stay taxable in the country that pays them.

## Who qualifies

Article 5B is open to individuals who:

1. **Receive a pension from abroad**, proven by a document from the foreign pension provider;
2. **Were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years**;
3. **Move their tax residence to Greece** from a state with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement on tax ([AADE decision A.1192/2026](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-09/a1192_2026fek.pdf), art. 3).

The law sets no nationality requirement and no minimum pension amount. The 5-of-6-years test is the same one used for the [50% tax break under Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/), which is for people who come to work. There is no family add-on: a spouse with their own foreign pension must qualify in their own right, and a spouse without one does not qualify; any foreign income they have is taxed under the normal rules. Someone already taxed under the non-dom regime (Article 5A) cannot switch to 5B, and vice versa (A.1192/2026, art. 3).

## What the 7% covers and what it does not

The 7% applies to **all foreign-source income**, not only the pension: foreign rent, dividends, interest and capital income are included. Paying the 7% settles your Greek tax on that foreign income for the year. Tax already paid abroad on that income is credited against the 7%, up to the Greek tax on it, where the tax treaty lets the source country tax the income or there is no treaty with it; any excess is not refunded (A.1192/2026, art. 5).

What stays outside:

- **Greek-source income** (Greek rent, a Greek job, Greek bank interest) is taxed under the normal rules.
- **Income a tax treaty exempts or taxes differently** follows the treaty. The common case is government pensions; see below.
- **Inheritance and gift tax** are not affected.

Note that the 7% applies even where income would otherwise be taxed lower. Foreign dividends, normally taxed at 5% in Greece, fall under the 7% too. If you still work or have large investment income, compare the other regimes in [Article 5C vs the non-dom regime](/guides/article-5c-vs-non-dom-regime/).

## Does it pay off?

Not always. The normal scale comes with a pension tax credit of up to €777, the 7% regime with none. For a single pensioner with no other income, the normal scale is cheaper up to roughly **€14,100** of foreign pension a year; above that the 7% wins, by about €3,000 a year at €30,000. Couples are tested per person, so two small pensions often gain little. The full comparison, with worked numbers and the break-even logic, is in [Greek pension tax for foreign retirees](/guides/greek-pension-tax-foreign-retirees/). Run it before you apply, because leaving the regime is final (see below).

## The 2026 changes: deadlines and payment

Article 94 of Law 5313/2026 and [AADE decision A.1192/2026](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-09/a1192_2026fek.pdf) (AADE is the Greek tax authority) reworked the procedure; AADE summarised it in its [press release of 25 September 2026](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-09/d.t._25.09.2026.pdf).

- **Application:** by **31 October** of the tax year you want the regime for, through myAADE (AADE's online portal, under "My Requests"), by post or courier, or in person. The competent office is KEFODE Attica or KEFODE Thessaloniki, depending on where you live.
- **Which year:** if you moved your tax residence to Greece **on or before 2 July**, you can choose that year (apply by 31 October of that year) or the following year. If you moved **after 2 July**, only the following year.
- **Documents:** proof of your pension from the foreign payer, and, for each of the previous years AADE has no record of, a tax residence certificate from your former country (or the treaty residence form, or a copy of your foreign tax assessment or return). Foreign public documents need an apostille or legalisation unless an exemption applies. Missing documents can follow until **30 November**; AADE decides by the last working day of December.
- **Duration:** the regime covers 15 tax years in total, starting with the year you apply for, and cannot be extended.
- **Payment:** the 7% is paid **once a year by the last working day of December**. There are no instalments. If you miss the payment, you are out of the regime from that year on and taxed on your worldwide income under the normal rules.
- **Leaving the regime:** you can revoke by 31 October of a tax year, but a revocation is final; you cannot come back in.
- **Annual return:** you still file a Greek income tax return (E1) every year, usually from mid-March to 15 July, declaring Greek and foreign income.

## Treaty pitfalls: which pension is taxed where

The 7% only bites on income Greece is allowed to tax. Each country's treaty with Greece decides that:

- **UK:** private, occupational and State Pensions are exempt in the UK once they are subject to Greek tax ([1953 convention](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a8015c1e5274a2e87db7c35/1953-greece-dtc_-_in_force.pdf), art. X); you claim this with HMRC's DT-Individual form (a 'No Tax' code stops UK deductions). **UK government pensions** (civil service, armed forces) stay taxable in the UK (art. VIII). More in our [UK guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-uk-tax-guide/).
- **Germany:** the statutory pension (gesetzliche Rente) is taxed only in Greece; **civil servant pensions** (Beamtenpensionen) stay taxable in Germany. More in our [Germany guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide/).
- **United States:** the US taxes its citizens wherever they live, so US citizens file in both countries; where Greece credits US tax, the credit is capped at the 7%, and whether US tax on your pension qualifies depends on the type of pension, so check before you apply. Non-US citizens receiving US Social Security face US withholding, since Greece is not on the [SSA list of treaty countries](https://secure.ssa.gov/POMS.NSF/lnx/0205010010) whose residents are exempt.
- **Canada:** Canada may also tax pensions at source, but the first CAD 15,000 a year is exempt and the tax on periodic payments above that is capped at 15% ([2009 treaty](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties/country/greece-convention-2009.html), art. 18); check how CPP and OAS are treated. Anything above the Greek 7% is not credited.
- **Australia:** there is no comprehensive tax treaty with Greece, only an [airline-profits agreement](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/foreign-income-tax-offset-rules-guide-2026/attachment-a-countries-and-jurisdictions-that-have-a-tax-treaty-with-australia), so double taxation is a real risk and the credit is capped at the Greek 7%.
- **Netherlands, Scandinavia and other state pensions:** check the specific treaty; some state pensions remain taxable at source.

How a treaty-exempt pension changes the 7%-versus-scale comparison is shown in the [pension tax guide](/guides/greek-pension-tax-foreign-retirees/).

## Residence and healthcare

**EU citizens** need no visa; staying longer than three months without working requires sufficient funds and health insurance, and you register with the police aliens department.

**Non-EU citizens, including the British**, need a national (type D) visa first and then a residence permit. The usual route for retirees is the **financially independent person** permit (amounts set by joint ministerial decision 225679/2024 under the Migration Code, Law 5038/2023): at least **€3,500 a month** net, plus 20% for a spouse and 15% per child (€4,200 for a couple), full health insurance, no right to work or business activity in Greece, valid three years and renewable. The Golden Visa (€800,000 in high-demand areas, €400,000 elsewhere, €250,000 for conversions) is the investment alternative. A residence permit does not by itself make you tax resident; see [the 183-day rule](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days/).

**Healthcare:** UK and EU state pensioners can usually request an **S1** form from their home country, get an AMKA (Greek social security number) and register the S1 with the Greek public health system (EOPYY; in practice often through your local EFKA office); the home country then pays for Greek public healthcare. The S1 can only be registered once you live here, so non-EU retirees usually bridge the first months with private cover, which the visa requires anyway. Pensioners with only a private pension, and those from the US, Canada, Australia and others, need private insurance (see [health insurance in Greece](/greece/health-insurance/)).

## Your first-year checklist

1. **AFM** (Greek tax number), online via myAADE; see [what the AFM is](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number/).
2. **Residence permit or EU registration**, and the move of your tax residence to Greece.
3. **AMKA and S1 registration** (if eligible), or private health insurance.
4. **Greek bank account**; see [opening a bank account](/guides/open-bank-account-greece/).
5. **Article 5B application** by 31 October, with the residence certificate and pension proof.
6. **DT-Individual or equivalent** in your home country, so the pension is not taxed twice.
7. **If you buy a home:** E9 (property register declaration) and ENFIA (the annual property tax); see [AFM for property buyers](/guides/afm-greece-property-buyers-non-residents/).
8. **Every year:** E1 return, usually mid-March to 15 July; 7% payment by the end of December.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming 7% always wins.** Below roughly €14,100 of foreign pension the normal scale is cheaper, and foreign dividends are taxed at 7% instead of 5%.
- **Assuming the spouse is covered.** Each spouse must qualify in their own right.
- **Treating the deadlines as soft.** A late application, a missed December payment or a hasty revocation cannot be fixed afterwards.
- **Relying on old guides** that still show the 31 March deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the 7% tax regime for pensioners in Greece?

A spouse has to qualify in their own right.

### What is the deadline to apply for the 7% regime?

### When is the 7% tax paid?
