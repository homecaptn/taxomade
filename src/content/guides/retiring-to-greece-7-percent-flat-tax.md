Greece taxes foreign pensioners who move here at a flat **7% on all their foreign income, for 15 years**. For a single pensioner with a mid-sized pension, that can save several thousand euros a year compared with the normal scale. The rules changed in 2026: the application deadline is now **31 October** of the tax year, not 31 March as older guides still say. This guide covers who qualifies, what it saves, the treaty traps and the rest of the move: residence, healthcare and the first-year admin.

## The short answer

- **7% flat on all foreign-source income** (pensions, but also foreign rent, dividends and interest) for **15 tax years**, under Article 5B of the Income Tax Code.
- **Conditions:** a pension from abroad, **not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years**, and a move from a country with a tax cooperation agreement with Greece (EU states and most countries with a tax treaty with Greece; check yours).
- **New deadline:** apply by **31 October** of the year you want the regime for; documents by 30 November; pay by the last working day of **December**, in one go.
- **Not automatic for couples:** each spouse qualifies and applies separately.
- **Government pensions** (UK civil service, German Beamte) usually stay taxable in the country that pays them.

## Who qualifies

Article 5B is open to individuals who:

1. **Receive a pension from abroad**, proven by a document from the foreign pension provider;
2. **Were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years**;
3. **Move their tax residence to Greece** from a state with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement on tax.

The law sets no nationality requirement and no minimum pension amount. The 5-of-6-years test is the same one used for the [50% tax break under Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c), which is for people who come to work. There is no family add-on: a spouse with their own foreign pension must qualify in their own right, and a spouse without one does not qualify; any foreign income they have is taxed under the normal rules.

## What the 7% covers and what it does not

The 7% applies to **all foreign-source income**, not only the pension: foreign rent, dividends, interest and capital income are included. Paying the 7% settles your Greek tax on that foreign income for the year. Tax already paid abroad on that income is credited against the 7%, up to the Greek tax on it, where the tax treaty lets the source country tax the income or there is no treaty with it; any excess is not refunded.

What stays outside:

- **Greek-source income** (Greek rent, a Greek job, Greek bank interest) is taxed under the normal rules.
- **Income a tax treaty exempts or taxes differently** follows the treaty. The common case is government pensions; see below.
- **Inheritance and gift tax** are not affected.

Note that the 7% applies even where income would otherwise be taxed lower. Foreign dividends, normally taxed at 5% in Greece, fall under the 7% too. If you still work or have large investment income, compare the other regimes in [Article 5C vs the non-dom regime](/guides/article-5c-vs-non-dom-regime).

## What it saves: a worked example

Single pensioner, foreign pension of **€30,000 a year**, tax year 2026:

| | Normal Greek scale | Article 5B |
|---|---|---|
| Tax on €30,000 | €5,500 (9% / 20% / 26% bands) | €2,100 (7%) |
| Pensioner tax credit | −€417 | – |
| **Tax due** | **≈ €5,083** (≈ 17%) | **€2,100** (7%) |

That is about **€3,000 a year**, or roughly €45,000 over 15 years at a constant pension. The benefit grows with the pension. For smaller pensions it shrinks fast: a couple with €15,000 each pays about €2,370 together on the normal scale and €2,100 under 5B, a saving of only around €270 a year. Run your own numbers before you apply, because the decision is hard to undo (see below).

## The 2026 changes: deadlines and payment

Law 5313/2026 and [AADE](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-09/a1192_2026fek.pdf) (the Greek tax authority) decision A.1192/2026 reworked the procedure. Older pages online still show a 31 March deadline; that no longer applies.

- **Application:** by **31 October** of the tax year you want the regime for, through myAADE (AADE's online portal, under "My Requests"), by post or in person at the competent tax office.
- **Which year:** if you moved your tax residence to Greece **on or before 2 July**, you can choose that year (apply by 31 October of that year) or the following year. If you moved **after 2 July**, only the following year.
- **Documents:** a tax residence certificate from your former country (or the treaty residence form where one applies) and proof of your pension from the foreign provider; foreign documents with an apostille. Missing documents can follow until **30 November**.
- **Payment:** the 7% is paid **once a year by the last working day of December**. There are no instalments. If you miss the payment, you are out of the regime from that year on and taxed on your worldwide income under the normal rules.
- **Leaving the regime:** you can revoke by 31 October of a tax year, but a revocation is final; you cannot come back in.
- **Annual return:** you still file a Greek income tax return (E1) every year, usually from mid-March to 15 July, declaring Greek and foreign income.

## Treaty pitfalls: which pension is taxed where

The 7% only bites on income Greece is allowed to tax. Each country's treaty with Greece decides that:

- **UK:** private and occupational pensions are taxed in Greece, and the UK State Pension is generally treated the same way; you claim UK exemption with HMRC's DT-Individual form (a 'No Tax' code stops UK deductions). **UK government pensions** (civil service, armed forces) stay taxable in the UK. More in our [UK guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-uk-tax-guide).
- **Germany:** the statutory pension (gesetzliche Rente) is taxed only in Greece; **civil servant pensions** (Beamtenpensionen) stay taxable in Germany. More in our [Germany guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide).
- **United States:** the treaty's saving clause lets the US keep taxing its citizens, who file in both countries; where Greece credits US tax, the credit is capped at the 7%, and whether US tax on your pension qualifies depends on the type of pension, so check before you apply. Non-US citizens receiving US Social Security face US withholding, since Greece is not on the US list of treaty exemptions.
- **Canada:** Canada may also tax private pensions at source, capped at 15% of the gross payment once annual payments exceed CAD 15,000; check how CPP and OAS are treated. Anything above the Greek 7% is not credited.
- **Australia:** there is no tax treaty with Greece, so double taxation is a real risk and the credit is capped at the Greek 7%.
- **Netherlands, Scandinavia and other state pensions:** check the specific treaty; some state pensions remain taxable at source.

## Residence and healthcare

**EU citizens** need no visa; staying longer than three months without working requires sufficient funds and health insurance, and you register with the police aliens department.

**Non-EU citizens, including the British**, need a national (type D) visa first and then a residence permit. The usual route for retirees is the **financially independent person** permit: at least **€3,500 a month** in stable income, plus 20% for a spouse and 15% per child (€4,200 for a couple), full health insurance, no right to work, valid three years and renewable. The Golden Visa (€800,000 in high-demand areas, €400,000 elsewhere, €250,000 for conversions) is the investment alternative. A residence permit does not by itself make you tax resident; see [the 183-day rule](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days).

**Healthcare:** UK and EU state pensioners can usually request an **S1** form from their home country, get an AMKA (Greek social security number) and register the S1 with the Greek public health system (EOPYY; in practice often through your local EFKA office); the home country then pays for Greek public healthcare. The S1 can only be registered once you live here, so non-EU retirees usually bridge the first months with private cover, which the visa requires anyway. Pensioners with only a private pension, and those from the US, Canada, Australia and others, need private insurance.

## Your first-year checklist

1. **AFM** (Greek tax number), online via myAADE; see [what the AFM is](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number).
2. **Residence permit or EU registration**, and the move of your tax residence to Greece.
3. **AMKA and S1 registration** (if eligible), or private health insurance.
4. **Greek bank account**; see [opening a bank account](/guides/open-bank-account-greece).
5. **Article 5B application** by 31 October, with the residence certificate and pension proof.
6. **DT-Individual or equivalent** in your home country, so the pension is not taxed twice.
7. **If you buy a home:** E9 (property register declaration) and ENFIA (the annual property tax); see [AFM for property buyers](/guides/afm-greece-property-buyers-non-residents).
8. **Every year:** E1 return, usually mid-March to 15 July; 7% payment by the end of December.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming 7% always wins.** On small pensions the saving is minimal, and foreign dividends are taxed at 7% instead of 5%.
- **Assuming the spouse is covered.** Each spouse must qualify in their own right.
- **Treating the deadlines as soft.** A late application, a missed December payment or a hasty revocation cannot be fixed afterwards.
- **Relying on old guides** that still show the 31 March deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the 7% tax regime for pensioners in Greece?

Under Article 5B of the Greek Income Tax Code, pensioners who move their tax residence to Greece can pay a flat 7% on all their foreign-source income for 15 tax years, instead of the normal progressive scale of 9% to 44%. Greek-source income is still taxed normally.

### Who qualifies for the 7% flat tax in Greece?

People who receive a pension from abroad, were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years, and move their tax residence to Greece from a country that has an administrative tax cooperation agreement with Greece, which covers EU states and most countries with a tax treaty with Greece. A spouse has to qualify in their own right.

### What is the deadline to apply for the 7% regime?

31 October of the tax year you want the regime for, with documents by 30 November. Tax residence moved to Greece by 2 July: that year or the next. Moved later: only the next year.

### When is the 7% tax paid?

Once a year, in one payment, by the last working day of December. No instalments; a missed payment takes you out of the regime from that year on.
