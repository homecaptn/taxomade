You bought a flat on Crete, got an AFM through a lawyer, and a "tax representative" appeared in the paperwork. Or your non-EU company wants to sell in Greece and an adviser says you need a "fiscal representative". The same English phrase covers two different legal roles, and the rules for the first changed in 2024.

This guide covers who needs a Greek tax representative (φορολογικός εκπρόσωπος) today, what that person actually does, and how to appoint or remove one. For the tax number itself, see [what a Greek AFM is](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number/).

## The short answer

- **Individuals and companies tax resident abroad:** a tax representative is **optional** since 9 September 2024, whether you live in the EU or not. Without one, AADE sends everything to the email and foreign address you declared.
- **Non-EU businesses registering for Greek VAT:** a **VAT tax agent** (φορολογικός αντιπρόσωπος) is generally still **mandatory**. EU businesses can register directly.
- **Liability:** the general tax representative carries none. The VAT agent is jointly liable for the VAT.
- **How:** through the AFM application or a registry change in myAADE (*My Requests*), with certified signatures from you and the representative.
- **Greek tax residents** don't need one. If you moved to Greece, the old representative should come off your record.

## The legal basis

The general rule is **Article 8 of the Tax Procedure Code** (ΚΦΔ, Law 5104/2024): a taxpayer who is tax resident abroad *may* appoint one Greek tax resident, a person or a company, as tax representative. The representative receives all AADE correspondence about you and handles formal matters such as registry updates. Under paragraph 3, they are not liable if your taxes go unfiled or unpaid.

The procedure is in **AADE decision [A.1069/2024](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2024-05/a1069_2024.pdf)** (ΦΕΚ Β' 2671/02.05.2024, amended by A.1125/2024), whose main provisions apply from **9 September 2024**. It replaced ΠΟΛ.1283/2013, under which non-residents with a Greek AFM had to have a representative. EU and non-EU residents are treated the same: an American with a holiday home and a German with a rental flat have the same choice.

## Do you need one? Typical cases

### Property owners and AFM holders living abroad

No legal obligation. If you skip the representative, you must declare your full foreign address, your foreign tax identification number and an email address, keep them current within 30 days of any change, and accept that AADE's notices arrive there.

The Athens office that used to handle non-residents (the former ΔΟΥ Κατοίκων Εξωτερικού) closed on 14 July 2025. Its work moved to a non-residents desk at the Attica tax service centre (ΚΕΦΟΔΕ Αττικής) in Tavros. Tax offices are still being merged, so your registry entry in myAADE shows which office is responsible for you.

Whether you *should* have one is a practical question: ENFIA notices and AADE queries arrive in Greek, on short deadlines, and missed deadlines bring automatic penalties and interest. The [property buyers guide](/guides/afm-greece-property-buyers-non-residents/) and the [rental income guide](/guides/rental-income-tax-greece-foreign-owners/) cover the filings that come with owning.

### Foreign companies with a Greek AFM

Also optional. A legal entity that needs a Greek AFM but doesn't want a representative registers and declares its legal representative (for example, the director), and AADE also sends correspondence to that person.

### Non-EU businesses registering for VAT

Here "fiscal representation" means something stricter. Under **Article 36(4)(δ) of the VAT Code** (Law 2859/2000), a business not established in Greece or anywhere else in the EU must appoint a VAT tax agent before making taxable supplies in Greece for which it, rather than the customer, owes the VAT. Article 8(2) of the Tax Procedure Code points to this rule for VAT.

The details are in ministerial decision ΠΟΛ.1281/1993, which still governs non-EU businesses:

- the agent must be VAT-registered in Greece, an individual or a company with its seat, a permanent establishment or its residence there, and must accept the appointment;
- you appoint them by power of attorney as your sole tax agent; one signed abroad needs an apostille or Greek consular certification, plus an official translation;
- the agent takes on your VAT obligations (registration, books, invoices, returns, payment) and is **jointly liable** with you for the VAT.

That liability is why VAT agents are harder to find, and more expensive, than a general tax representative.

**EU businesses** can get a Greek VAT number directly and may use an agent only if they prefer. Where the customer self-accounts under the reverse charge, there is often no Greek registration at all. Non-EU sellers of digital services to consumers usually use the EU's non-Union OSS scheme instead of a Greek registration. Check each case before appointing anyone.

## Who can be a representative

For the general (non-VAT) role: any **individual or company that is tax resident in Greece**, one at a time and only with their written acceptance. Usually that is a relative, a lawyer or an accountant. The role covers receiving mail and formal matters. It is not a power of attorney: it doesn't let them sign contracts or sell property for you, and an accountant who files your returns needs a separate authorisation.

## How to appoint one

### With a new AFM

The appointment goes into the AFM application itself. If a lawyer or accountant is applying for your AFM under a power of attorney, they can add it in the same step.

### If you already have an AFM

1. File a **registry change** (μεταβολή στοιχείων) in myAADE under *My Requests* (Τα Αιτήματά μου) → Registry, using form Δ210 for individuals or Δ211 for entities.
2. Attach a **declaration appointing the representative**, with your signature certified. You can do this as a solemn declaration via gov.gr with your own TAXISnet codes, at a KEP or Greek consulate, or by notarial power of attorney.
3. Attach the representative's **written acceptance**, also with a certified signature.
4. State where notices should go: to the representative only, or to you and the representative.

Documents signed abroad need an **apostille** (or consular certification) and an official Greek translation. If someone files the request for you, they need an authorisation with your certified signature. Any change must be declared within **30 days**.

### What it costs

AADE charges nothing for the appointment. What you pay is the representative's fee, and there is no standard market rate: relatives do it for free, while lawyers and accountants usually price it into a package with the annual return, E9 and ENFIA checks. A VAT agent for a non-EU business costs considerably more, because of the joint liability.

## How to end it

- **You end it:** declare the change in myAADE (*My Requests*) within 30 days, either naming a new representative or confirming you want notices at your own contact details.
- **The representative withdraws:** they can do so unilaterally in myAADE if you haven't declared the change yourself. AADE then notifies you. You have 30 days to appoint someone new or opt for your own contact details. If you do nothing, AADE treats you as having chosen your own contact details.

Either way, first check that your email and foreign address in myAADE are current.

## Common mistakes

### The old representative stays on after you move to Greece

The most frequent one. You got your AFM as a non-resident, often with an accountant or relative as representative, then moved to Greece. Your residence in the register doesn't change by itself. Until you file the residence change (*My Requests → Registry*, form Δ210, or through the 5A/5B/5C application if you use one of those regimes), you stay a non-resident on paper: the representative may keep receiving your notices, and you can't get a [tax residence certificate](/guides/greek-tax-residency-certificate/). Make sure the representative entry goes with the same change. If you've been in Greece a while without sorting this out, start with the [fix-it checklist](/guides/living-in-greece-without-tax-setup/).

### Not knowing who your representative is

If an accountant got your AFM years ago, check your registry details in myAADE. Some people find a former accountant, an agent or an ex-partner still listed, with nobody forwarding the post.

### Assuming the representative files for you

They receive mail and handle formal matters. They don't file your E1 or E9 unless they're also engaged and authorised to do so, and they're not liable if nobody does.

### Thinking a sale needs a representative

It doesn't, since 2024. What [selling as a non-resident](/guides/selling-property-greece-non-resident/) does need is working TAXISnet access, filed E9 and ENFIA history, and someone in Greece with a power of attorney if you won't be there to sign.
