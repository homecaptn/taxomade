If you run a business or work as a freelancer in Greece, you've probably seen the phrase "mandatory e-invoicing" a lot lately. It sounds technical. The idea behind it is simple, though: for certain invoices, *how* you issue them is now regulated. For most businesses within the scope of the new rules, including freelancers, that starts on October 1, 2026.

Here's what it means in plain language.

## The short answer

- **What it is:** For invoices covered by the new rules, you can no longer just create an invoice in Word, Excel or any invoicing tool and send it. It has to be issued through an approved channel, which transmits the data to the Greek tax authority (AADE).
- **Who it affects:** Businesses established in Greece. Large businesses (over €1 million revenue in 2023) started in March 2026. All other businesses within the scope of the rules, freelancers included, follow from October 1, 2026.
- **Which invoices:** Invoices to Greek businesses and to businesses outside the EU. Invoices to businesses in other EU countries are optional for now, and invoices to private customers aren't part of this mandate.
- **Transition period:** October 1 to December 31, 2026, for businesses that have filed the required declaration with AADE.

## What "e-invoicing" actually means

Most people assume an invoice sent by email is already an "electronic invoice." Under the Greek mandate, that's not the point.

What matters is **how the invoice is issued**. An e-invoice in this sense is created through an approved channel that transmits the invoice data to AADE's **myDATA** platform, where the document receives a unique registration number, called a **MARK**.

A PDF you created yourself in Word, Excel or an ordinary invoicing tool that isn't connected to an approved channel no longer meets the requirement for invoices in scope.

## Didn't Greece already have myDATA?

Yes, and this is where many people get confused.

myDATA isn't new. Greek businesses have been reporting their invoice data to AADE through myDATA for several years. What changes now is the step before that: for invoices covered by the mandate, the invoice itself must be issued through an approved channel, and the transmission to myDATA is part of issuing it.

## How to issue e-invoices: your three options

AADE allows three routes:

1. **An AADE-approved e-invoicing provider.** Private companies approved by AADE. Your existing accounting software or ERP can often be integrated with an approved provider, so you keep your software while the provider handles the compliant issuance. On its own, ordinary accounting software isn't an approved channel.
2. **timologio.** A free web application from AADE. Basic, but enough for most freelancers and small businesses with a handful of invoices per month.
3. **myDATAapp.** AADE's free mobile app.

Before you start, you (or your accountant) must also file a declaration with AADE stating which channel you'll use.

## Which of your invoices are affected

This is the part that matters most for internationals, because many freelancers in Greece have clients abroad.

One detail for non-EU clients: a US or UK company may not be set up to receive a Greek e-invoice. If a non-EU client doesn't accept electronic invoices, AADE allows the invoice to be delivered to them in another way, under certain conditions. It must still be issued through the approved channel; only the delivery can change. Your accountant can check whether this applies to your client.

So a developer who works only for a German company will see little change for now. A consultant with Greek clients, or a designer billing a US agency, needs a compliant setup.

## What the transition period means

From October 1 to December 31, 2026, businesses can move to the new system gradually and keep using their existing invoicing setup alongside it.

This isn't automatic. It depends on having filed the required declaration with AADE. If you haven't done that yet, it's the first thing to do, and there's no reason to wait.

From January 1, 2027, invoices covered by the mandate must be issued through the approved channels.

## What happens if you don't comply

An invoice that should have been issued through an approved channel but wasn't can be treated as if no invoice was issued. That triggers penalties under the Greek Tax Procedure Code:

- **Invoices with VAT:** 50% of the VAT amount involved, with a minimum of **€250** for businesses with single-entry books or **€500** with double-entry books.
- **Invoices without VAT:** fixed fines of **€500** (single-entry) or **€1,000** (double-entry).
- **Repeat violations** lead to higher penalties.

In other words: even one small invoice issued the wrong way can cost more than a compliant setup, especially when AADE's own tools are free.

## How Greece compares to other countries

Greece isn't an outlier. Several EU countries have already moved to mandatory e-invoicing, or are about to:

- **Italy** has required B2B e-invoicing since 2019, the longest-running system in Europe.
- **Belgium** made it mandatory for B2B invoices from January 2026.
- **Germany** has required businesses to be able to receive e-invoices since 2025, with the obligation to issue them phasing in over the next years.

The systems differ technically. Greece and Italy route invoice data through the tax authority's own platform, while Belgium uses a network (Peppol) where invoices travel directly between businesses.

At EU level, the "VAT in the Digital Age" (ViDA) reforms are set to extend e-invoicing and digital reporting to B2B transactions between EU countries from July 2030. That's when the "optional" row in the table above is expected to change.

## What to do now

1. **Check who your clients are.** If any are Greek businesses or businesses outside the EU, you're in scope.
2. **Choose your channel.** For low volume, AADE's free timologio usually works. For higher volume or software integration, an AADE-approved e-invoicing provider.
3. **File the declaration** with AADE.
4. **Use the transition period** to test before January 2027.

## FAQ

### Is e-invoicing mandatory in Greece?

Yes, for invoices covered by the rules. Under Law 5222/2025, businesses with over €1 million revenue in 2023 started in March 2026. All other businesses in scope follow from October 1, 2026, with a transition period until December 31, 2026.

### Does e-invoicing apply to freelancers in Greece?

Yes. Freelancers and sole traders fall under the second phase from October 1, 2026, for invoices to Greek businesses and to businesses outside the EU.

### Is a PDF invoice enough in Greece?

Not for invoices covered by the mandate. A PDF created in Word, Excel or an ordinary invoicing tool doesn't meet the requirement. The invoice must be issued through an AADE-approved e-invoicing provider, timologio or myDATAapp.

### Do I need e-invoicing for clients in other EU countries?

Not currently. For invoices to businesses in other EU countries, e-invoicing is optional. This is expected to change under the EU's ViDA reforms from July 2030.

### Is there a free e-invoicing tool in Greece?

Yes. AADE offers timologio, a free web application, and myDATAapp, a free mobile app.

### What is the penalty for not using e-invoicing in Greece?

A non-compliant invoice can be treated as not issued. For invoices with VAT, the penalty is 50% of the VAT, with a minimum of €250 (single-entry books) or €500 (double-entry books). Without VAT, fixed fines of €500 or €1,000 apply. Repeat violations are penalized more heavily.
