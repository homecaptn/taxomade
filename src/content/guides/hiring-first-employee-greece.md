Your first hire in Greece is a bigger step than it looks from abroad. The salary is the smallest surprise; the 14 payments, the employer contributions, and a digital filing system that fines you €10,500 for one undeclared day are the ones that catch founders out. This guide covers what a sole proprietor or IKE owner has to do and pay in 2026, with a worked example.

## The short answer

- **Cost:** budget about **17 times the monthly gross** per year. €1,500 gross a month costs roughly €25,600 a year.
- **Why:** 14 salaries (Christmas bonus, Easter bonus, leave allowance) plus **21.79% employer EFKA** on each. The employee pays 13.37%.
- **Minimum wage:** €920 gross a month since 1 April 2026.
- **Before day one:** employer registration with e-EFKA, then the hiring declaration in **ERGANI II**, accepted by the employee, before the first working hour.
- **Digital work card:** mandatory for IT, consulting, advertising and office businesses from 16 November 2026.
- **First 12 months:** termination without notice or severance; probation up to six months.

## 1. Register as an employer

**Sole proprietor:** you register yourself as an employer on e-EFKA through gov.gr and receive an employer number (ΑΜΕ). It is online only, free, and asks for each employee's AFM, AMKA, specialty and start date. You need to be registered with AADE and insured with EFKA yourself first. Details on the setup are in [how to register as a freelancer in Greece](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece).

**IKE:** a company formed through the GEMI one-stop-shop gets its employer number automatically at incorporation. Note that the IKE's managing partner is insured as self-employed, not as an employee; your first hire is usually the first person on the company's payroll. The company side is covered in [setting up an IKE](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs).

## 2. ERGANI II: file before the first working hour

ERGANI II, the Ministry of Labour's employment register, has been fully live since 16 February 2026. Three rules matter for a first hire:

- **Hiring declaration before work starts.** The digital hiring declaration (which replaced the old E3 form) is filed before the employee's first working hour, and the employee accepts it in the MyErgani app, again before starting. A trial day without it counts as undeclared work.
- **Contract terms on file.** The main terms of employment are posted in ERGANI II before hiring and before any change. Salary changes are declared within 30 days.
- **Terminations** are declared in ERGANI II as well, including the end of a probation period.

The fine for undeclared work is **€10,500 per worker**, reduced to €7,000, €5,000 or €3,000 if you then hire the person full-time for 3, 6 or 12 months.

### The digital work card

The digital work card records each employee's actual start and end of work in real time. It already covers about two million workers in industry, retail, banking and tourism. In 2026 it extends to new sectors: health, cleaning and telecoms from 12 October 2026, and **IT, consulting, advertising, office administration and call centres from 16 November 2026**. You cannot opt in early. If the card does not match the hours actually worked, the fine is again €10,500 per employee.

## 3. What you pay: wages and contributions

**Minimum wage:** €920 gross a month for employees (€41.09 a day for manual workers) from 1 April 2026. A further increase from 1 April 2027 has been announced, with a target of €1,000 by January 2028; the 2027 amount has not been set yet.

**Social security (EFKA)**, standard private-sector rates for 2026:

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Employer | 21.79% |
| Employee (withheld from pay) | 13.37% |
| Total | 35.16% |

Contributions are calculated on gross pay up to €7,761.94 a month. Payroll declaration (ΑΠΔ) and payment are due by the last working day of the following month; when the declaration deadline is extended, the payment deadline is not. A further 0.5-point cut on the employee side has been announced from 1 April 2027.

**Income tax withholding** follows the 2026 scale (9% to 44%, [Law 5246/2025](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5246/2025/arthro/3)), with lower rates for employees under 30 and for parents. Payroll works out the annual tax, deducts the employee tax credit and withholds one fourteenth each month.

## 4. Fourteen salaries, not twelve

Greek employees are paid 14 times a year:

- **Christmas bonus:** about one month's salary, paid by **21 December**.
- **Easter bonus:** about half a month's salary, paid by **Holy Wednesday**.
- **Leave allowance:** up to half a month's salary, paid when the leave starts.

With the small leave-related top-ups, the total comes to about 14.06 monthly salaries. Employer EFKA applies to all of them.

**Annual leave** on a five-day week is 20 working days in the first year, 21 in the second and 22 from the third, rising further with seniority.

## 5. Worked example: €1,500 gross a month

Employee aged 31 or over, no children, 2026 rates, simplified to 14 equal payments:

| | Per year | Per payment |
|---|---|---|
| Gross pay (14 × €1,500) | €21,000 | €1,500 |
| Employer EFKA (21.79%) | €4,576 | €327 |
| **Total cost to you** | **≈ €25,600** | |
| Employee EFKA (13.37%) | €2,808 | €201 |
| Income tax withheld | ≈ €1,885 | ≈ €135 |
| **Employee net pay** | **≈ €16,300** | **≈ €1,165** |

Rule of thumb: **total cost ≈ 17 × monthly gross**, net pay ≈ 78% of gross. An employee under 25 pays no income tax on the first €20,000 and takes home about €1,300 per payment on the same salary. Not included: overtime, occupational-risk surcharges, work-card equipment and the accountant's payroll fee.

## 6. Probation and dismissal

A probation period of **up to six months** can be agreed. During the **first 12 months** of an open-ended contract, either side can terminate without notice and without severance, unless agreed otherwise; probation counts towards those 12 months. After that, severance applies, starting at two months' pay for one to four years of service when dismissed without notice (half that amount with notice). Every termination is declared in ERGANI II.

## 7. Hiring people from abroad

- **EU citizens** need no work permit, only an AFM and AMKA (and a registration certificate after three months).
- **Non-EU citizens** need a residence permit with the right to work, or an employer-led invitation procedure. Since Law 5275/2026 the minimum contract for invited workers is six months, and decisions are due within 90 days.
- **Digital Nomad Visa holders** cannot work for a Greek employer; the visa only covers work for employers and clients outside Greece.
- **Someone living in another country** generally falls under that country's employment and social security rules; for one or two people abroad, an Employer of Record is often simpler.

### Hiring someone under Article 5C

If your new employee moves to Greece and qualifies for [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) (not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years), they hand you the AADE approval naming your company, and payroll then withholds income tax on only 50% of their salary. EFKA stays on the full gross. That is a real recruiting argument, but only with the approval in hand; the employee side is explained in [5C for employees and remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers).

## Common mistakes

- **A "trial day" before the ERGANI declaration.** That is undeclared work, at €10,500.
- **Budgeting 12 salaries.** It is 14, plus about 22% on top.
- **Paying late because the ΑΠΔ deadline moved.** The payment deadline did not.
- **Missing the work card** in IT, consulting or office businesses from 16 November 2026.
- **Paying someone full-time on freelance invoices** to avoid payroll. That can be reclassified as employment, with contributions due retroactively.
