Freelancers, remote workers, BV owners and retirees moving from the Netherlands all land in the same Greek system. What differs is the Dutch exit. This guide covers the Dutch side and points you to the right Greek setup.

## The short answer

- **Deregister** from the BRP if you leave for more than 8 months, no earlier than 5 days before departure.
- **The year you move** is filed with an M return, split into a resident and a non-resident period.
- **The 1981 treaty:** freelance income and pensions are taxed in Greece, Dutch property and government pensions stay Dutch.
- **AOW stops building up**, 2% less per year abroad, unless you take voluntary insurance within one year.
- **BV owners and pension holders** receive a protective exit assessment (conserverende aanslag).
- **Retirees** with a Dutch pension get Greek state healthcare through the CAK and an S1.
- **Article 5C** (50% off income tax for 7 years) is open to Dutch movers who meet the conditions.

## 1. Before you leave: deregistration

If you will live abroad for more than 8 months within one year, you [deregister from the Personal Records Database (BRP)](https://www.government.nl/faq/when-should-i-deregister-from-the-personal-records-database), in person at your municipality and **no earlier than 5 days before you leave**. Keeping your Dutch home does not change this.

Your record moves to the **RNI**, the register of non-residents, and the BRP passes your new address to the Belastingdienst and your pension funds. In Greece, residence for tax purposes follows the [183-day rule and your centre of life](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days).

Also on the list:

- **DigiD:** activate the app and ID check [before you go](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/emigreren-checklist); you need it for the M return.
- **Toeslagen and voorlopige aanslag:** both are based on your Dutch situation; stop or adjust them.
- **KVK:** with no business activity left in the Netherlands, you [report that the Dutch business stops](https://www.kvk.nl/veilig-zakendoen/zzpen-vanuit-het-buitenland-5-voorbeelden/). Dutch clients don't require a KVK registration.

## 2. The year you move: the M return

In the year you emigrate, you file an [M return (M-aangifte)](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/deels-niet-in-nederland-wonen-m-aangifte-doen), online through Mijn Belastingdienst or on the paper M form. The year is split into a period as a Dutch resident and a period as a non-resident.

From the following year, the Netherlands only taxes your Dutch income. [Dutch real estate is always taxed there](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/wonen-in-het-buitenland-nederlands-inkomen): a rented flat or holiday home goes into box 3 on your non-resident return, together with its mortgage. Income the treaty assigns to Greece, such as your pension, is exempted in that return.

## 3. The double tax treaty

The Netherlands and Greece use the [convention signed in Athens on 16 July 1981](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/trb-1981-178.pdf). A 2006 protocol changed nothing for individuals.

| Income | Taxed in |
|---|---|
| Freelance work done from Greece | Greece, unless you keep a fixed base in the Netherlands |
| Employment | Where the work is physically done; Dutch workdays can stay Dutch |
| Director's fees from a Dutch BV | The Netherlands may tax them; Greece credits |
| Dutch property | The Netherlands (box 3); Greece credits |
| Private and occupational pensions | Greece; lump sums for past Dutch work may also be taxed in the Netherlands |
| AOW, WIA and other social benefits | Greece |
| Government pensions (often via ABP) | The Netherlands, unless you are a Greek national living in Greece |
| Dutch dividends | The Netherlands withholds up to 15%; Greece credits |
| Gains on Dutch company shares | The Netherlands may tax them for 5 years after you leave |

If both countries claim you as resident, your permanent home decides first, then where your personal and economic ties are closer.

## 4. Freelancers and remote workers

As a zzp'er, you register as a sole proprietor in Greece, join EFKA and invoice from your Greek business. Your Dutch clients stay your clients; invoices to Dutch businesses go out under the reverse charge ([VAT with foreign clients](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients)).

If you [work permanently in another EU country](https://www.svb.nl/nl/id/u-werkt-buiten-nederland/bekijk-of-u-verzekerd-blijft-in-nederland), you are insured there, so with EFKA. New freelancers pay a reduced €150.46 per month plus €10 unemployment contribution; the standard lowest class is €250.77. You register by the 10th of the month after you start ([EFKA guide](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece)).

Expect a tax advance (27.5% in year one, then 55%), monthly VAT for 24 months, and e-invoicing from 2 November 2026. The full setup is in [registering as a freelancer in Greece](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece), starting with your [AFM](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number).

If you were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years, [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) exempts 50% of your income from Greek income tax for 7 years. Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible.

## 5. Employees of a Dutch employer

If you keep your Dutch contract and work from Greece, salary for work done in Greece is taxed in Greece. The treaty's short-stay exemption doesn't cover pay from an employer resident in the Netherlands, so days you physically work there can stay Dutch.

Permanent work from Greece also puts you under EFKA; only a temporary posting with an A1 certificate keeps you in Dutch social insurance. Article 5C as an employee needs a Greek employer or Greek branch; see [5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers).

## 6. BV owners

**Conserverende aanslag.** When you emigrate with a substantial interest (aanmerkelijk belang) in a company, the [Belastingdienst treats your shares as sold](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/conserverende-aanslag-bij-emigratie) and issues a protective box 2 assessment. For EU moves, payment is deferred automatically and interest-free. For emigrations since 15 September 2015, the assessment never lapses: it becomes (partly) payable if you sell the shares, receive dividends or the company stops. Moving on to a non-EU country ends the deferral.

**Director's fees and management.** The treaty lets the Netherlands tax board fees from a Dutch company even when you live in Greece. A company's residence follows its place of effective management, so running a BV alone from Greece can move it. For a new Greek company, see [setting up an IKE](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs).

## 7. Retirees

**Pensions.** Your occupational pension and AOW are taxed in Greece. To stop Dutch tax being withheld, you apply to the Belastingdienst for an [exemption from Dutch wage tax](https://www.svb.nl/en/general/general/exemption-from-tax). If you live abroad when you reach AOW age, you [apply yourself](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/retiring), ideally six months ahead.

**Exit assessment.** Pension and annuity rights built up with tax relief also receive a conserverende aanslag. You pay nothing as long as you follow Dutch rules, for example by not buying out (afkopen) the pension; after 10 years you can request remission.

**Health insurance.** If you receive a Dutch statutory pension and no longer work, you [register with the CAK](https://hetcak.nl/en/health-insurance-abroad/pension-benefit/registering/procedure), up to 5 months ahead. You receive an S1 to register with EOPYY, the Greek state insurer; the CAK deducts a monthly contribution from your pension.

**Greek side.** Pensioners who were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years can apply for a 7% flat tax on foreign income for 15 years, by 31 October (A.1192/2026). Otherwise the scale of 9% to 44% applies. Details in [retiring to Greece: the 7% flat tax](/guides/retiring-to-greece-7-percent-flat-tax).

## 8. AOW, benefits and health cover for working-age movers

Abroad, your AOW is [reduced by 2% for each uninsured year](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/uitkering-meenemen-naar-buitenland/vraag-en-antwoord/bouw-ik-aow-op-als-ik-in-het-buitenland-ga-wonen-of-werken). You can apply to the SVB for voluntary AOW and Anw insurance **within one year** of leaving, if you were [insured for the full year before](https://www.svb.nl/en/vv/living-working-outside-the-netherlands/conditions-for-voluntary-insurance), for a [maximum of 10 years](https://www.svb.nl/nl/vv/wonen-werken-buiten-nederland/hoelang-kunt-u-zich-vrijwillig-verzekeren). Ask the SVB how it combines with EFKA.

A [WIA, WAO or WAZ benefit](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/buitenland/uitkering-meenemen-buitenland/wia-wao-waz) can go with you within the EU. Tell UWV at least 2 weeks before you leave; a WGA benefit abroad is not always the same amount.

Dutch basic health insurance is for people who [live or work in the Netherlands](https://www.zorginstituutnederland.nl/verzekerde-zorg/b/buitenland-en-zorg-zvw). Give your insurer your departure date, ideally two months ahead. In Greece, EFKA contributions give you state healthcare.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to deregister from my Dutch municipality when I move to Greece?

Yes, if you will live abroad for more than 8 months within a year. You deregister in person, no earlier than 5 days before you leave. Your record then moves to the RNI, the register of non-residents.

### Is my Dutch pension taxed in Greece or in the Netherlands?

Under the 1981 treaty, private and occupational pensions and AOW are taxed in Greece once you live there. Government service pensions, often paid through ABP, stay taxable in the Netherlands unless you are a Greek national living in Greece.

### Do I keep building up AOW if I move to Greece?

No. Your AOW is reduced by 2% for each year you live and work abroad. You can apply to the SVB for voluntary insurance within one year of leaving, for up to 10 years.

### What is the Dutch conserverende aanslag when you emigrate?

A protective assessment on pension rights built up with tax relief and, for owners of a substantial interest in a BV, on a deemed sale of the shares. Within the EU, payment is deferred automatically; it becomes due on events such as buying out the pension, selling the shares or receiving dividends.

### Can I use the Greek 50% tax break (Article 5C) when moving from the Netherlands?

Yes, if you were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years and take a job with a Greek employer or register a business in Greece. Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible.

*Rules as of October 2026. Dutch and Greek rules change; check the current figures before you act.*
