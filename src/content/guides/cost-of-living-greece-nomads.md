Greece is often sold as a cheap paradise. It is more affordable than London, New York or Paris, but tourism and remote workers have changed the market.

To plan your relocation you need realistic numbers. Here is a breakdown of the cost of living in Greece for a single remote worker in 2026.

## The short answer

- **Single remote worker, comfortable:** €1,500 to €1,900 per month in Athens, Thessaloniki or Crete.
- **Biggest variable:** rent. €600 to €1,000 for a furnished one-bedroom, €450 to €650 on a local unfurnished lease.
- **Freelancers add EFKA:** about €160 per month in the first five years, €260 and more after.
- **Winter costs more than you think:** poorly insulated buildings, heating and electricity.
- **Islands:** rents drop sharply in winter, but so does everything else.

## Estimated monthly budget (single person)

Expect your costs to fall between **€1,200 and €1,900 per month** all-in. Around €1,200 is possible on an unfurnished local lease outside the centre, in Thessaloniki or on Crete; a comfortable setup with a furnished flat, coworking and EFKA in Athens is closer to €1,800.

### 1. Accommodation: €600 to €1,000

Housing is your biggest variable. Unfurnished local leases run roughly €450 to €650 (Thessaloniki or outside the Athens centre at the low end, central Athens around €600 and up), but most remote workers look for furnished, move-in-ready apartments via Airbnb, Spotahome or local agents.

- In Athens, Crete or Thessaloniki, expect **€600 to €1,000** for a modern, furnished one-bedroom in a good neighbourhood.
- Tourist hotspots like Mykonos or Santorini demand €1,300 and more and are heavily inflated in summer.
- Deposits, agent fees and the lease registration are covered in our [renting guide](/guides/renting-apartment-greece-foreigner); for choosing a city, see [Athens vs. Thessaloniki vs. the islands](/guides/athens-vs-thessaloniki-digital-nomads).

### 2. Groceries and supermarkets: €200 to €350

Supermarket prices for packaged goods and dairy are comparable to the rest of Western Europe. You save by buying fresh produce, meat and fish at the weekly farmers' markets (*laiki agora*).

### 3. Eating out: €150 to €300

Dining out is part of life here and still affordable.

- A quick local meal (gyros or souvlaki pita): **€4 to €10**
- A sit-down dinner at a traditional taverna, with house wine: **€12 to €20**
- A modern restaurant or brunch spot: **€15 to €30**
- A freddo espresso at a café: **€2 to €4**

### 4. Transport: €30 to €50

Public transport in Athens is cheap and effective.

- 30-day unlimited card for all modes: **€27** (annual €300)
- Single metro or bus ticket (90 minutes): **€1.20**
- Taxis via FREENOW or Uber (which books taxis in Greece) are affordable for short distances.

### 5. Utilities and internet: €80 to €150

Electricity, water, building charges (*koinochrista*, the shared costs of the block) and fibre internet (about €25 to €35) usually run €80 to €150 a month. Budget the top of that range in winter if your building relies on A/C or oil heating, and in summer if you run the A/C daily.

### 6. Coworking spaces: €150 to €250

A hot desk at an Athens coworking space typically costs €150 to €250 per month; smaller cities are cheaper. Prices vary by location and season.

### 7. Health insurance (visa requirement): €30 to €100

To hold a Digital Nomad Visa, you must carry private health insurance valid in Greece. Visa-compliant basic policies covering hospitalisation and emergencies start around €30 to €60 per month depending on your age; broader plans run €50 to €100 and more.

### 8. Social security (EFKA) for freelancers: from €160

If you register as a freelancer (sole proprietor) in Greece, whether or not you qualify for the Article 5C tax break, you must pay social security (EFKA). In 2026 the lowest regular class is **€250.77 per month**. New freelancers can apply for a special class of **€150.46 per month** for their first five years. Most freelancers pay a €10 unemployment contribution on top. Details in our [EFKA guide](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece). Note that Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible for 5C; see [5C vs. the Digital Nomad Visa](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa).

### 9. Income tax: the part most budgets forget

Income tax is not a monthly line, but it is real money. Freelance profit is taxed on a progressive scale from 9% to 44%, and advance tax for the following year is collected with your first return. If you moved your tax residency to Greece and were not a Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years, [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) halves the taxable base. The full picture is in our [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026).

## The reality of seasons

- **The winter heating trap.** Winters can be surprisingly cold, especially in Thessaloniki (grey from November to March) and Athens. Many older buildings are poorly insulated and rely on oil heating or A/C units. Expats routinely underestimate winter electricity bills.
- **Island seasonality.** Rents on islands like Crete or Rhodes can drop sharply, often 30 to 50%, in winter (November to April). You pay for the discount with closed businesses, limited social life and reduced ferry and flight connections.
- **The sweet spot.** May, June and September offer the best balance: warm weather, manageable crowds, normal prices.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to live in Greece as a digital nomad?

Budget €1,200 to €1,900 per month as a single remote worker in Athens, Thessaloniki or Crete. Rent is the biggest item; freelancers add about €160 per month in EFKA.

### Is Greece cheap for remote workers?

Cheaper than London, Paris or New York, but no longer a bargain. The savings come from rent outside tourist hotspots, tavernas, farmers' markets and public transport.

### How much is rent in Athens for a furnished apartment?

€600 to €1,000 per month for a modern, furnished one-bedroom in a good neighbourhood. Unfurnished local leases run roughly €450 to €650.

### Do digital nomads pay social security in Greece?

Only if you register as a freelancer in Greece: about €160 per month in the first five years. Remote employees working from Greece full-time are normally insured in Greece too, unless they are on a posting with an A1 certificate.

## Summary

Greece offers good value, especially outside the tourist islands. Budget around €1,500 a month in Athens or Thessaloniki and you live well, with room for weekend trips. If you are registering a business here, our [ongoing bookkeeping](/greece/ongoing-bookkeeping) starts at €89 per month with myDATA, VAT returns and the annual tax return included, so the tax side of your budget is a fixed number too.
