Greece is often marketed as a cheap paradise. While it is certainly more affordable than London, New York, or Paris, the influx of tourism and remote workers has changed the market. 

To plan your relocation, you need realistic numbers, not sugarcoated estimates. Here is a breakdown of the true cost of living in Greece for a single remote worker.

## Estimated Monthly Budget (Single Person)

Expect your baseline costs to fall between **€1,000 and €1,600 per month**. While €1,000 is doable on a strict budget (especially in Thessaloniki or Crete), a comfortable lifestyle is closer to €1,500+.

### 1. Accommodation: €600 – €1,000
Finding housing is your biggest variable. While long-term, unfurnished local leases can be found for €350–€500, most digital nomads seek furnished, move-in-ready apartments (via Airbnb, Spotahome, or local agents). 
- In Athens, Crete, or Thessaloniki, expect to pay **€600 to €1,000** for a modern, furnished 1-bedroom apartment in a good neighborhood.
- *Warning:* Tourist hotspots like Mykonos or Santorini will demand €1,300+ and are heavily inflated during summer.

### 2. Groceries & Supermarkets: €200 – €350
Supermarket prices for packaged goods and dairy are comparable to the rest of Western Europe. However, you can save significantly by buying fresh produce, meat, and fish at the weekly local farmers' markets (*laiki agora*). 

### 3. Eating Out: €150 – €300
Dining out in Greece is culturally essential and highly affordable.
- A quick local meal (gyros/souvlaki pita): **€4 – €10**
- A sit-down dinner at a traditional taverna (with house wine): **€12 – €20**
- A modern western-style restaurant or brunch spot: **€15 – €30**
- A daily *freddo espresso* at a café: **€2 – €4**

### 4. Transport: €30 – €50
Public transport is cheap and effective in Athens.
- A monthly unlimited transit pass in Athens: **~€30**
- A single metro/bus ticket: **~€1.20**
- Taxis (via apps like FREENOW or Uber/taxi) are affordable for short distances.

### 5. Coworking Spaces: €150+
If you prefer an office environment, a hot desk at a local coworking space generally starts around €150 per month. Note that prices vary by location and change seasonally. 

### 6. Health Insurance (Visa Requirement): €30 – €60
To hold a Digital Nomad Visa, you must carry private health insurance authorized in Greece. Basic policies covering hospitalization and emergencies typically cost between €30 and €60 per month depending on your age.

### 7. Social Security (EFKA) for Freelancers: €220
If you are registered as a local freelancer (sole proprietor) to take advantage of the Article 5C tax break, you must pay mandatory social security (EFKA). The baseline tier is roughly **€220 per month** (discounted to ~€130 for the first five years of a new business).

## The Reality of Seasons

When budgeting for Greece, you must factor in the seasons:

- **The Winter Heating Trap:** Winters can be surprisingly cold, especially in Thessaloniki (grey from November to March) and Athens. Many older Greek buildings are poorly insulated and rely on expensive oil heating or A/C units. Electricity bills in winter are often heavily underestimated by expats.
- **Island Seasonality:** Rents on islands like Crete or Rhodes can literally halve during the winter (November–April). However, you pay for this discount with closed businesses, limited social life, and reduced ferry/flight connections.
- **The Sweet Spot:** May, June, and September offer the best balance. The weather is warm, the crowds are manageable, and prices are normal.

## Summary

Greece offers immense value for money, incredible food, and a top-tier lifestyle. By budgeting around €1,500 a month, you can enjoy the best of the country without financial stress, leaving plenty of room for weekend trips to the islands.
