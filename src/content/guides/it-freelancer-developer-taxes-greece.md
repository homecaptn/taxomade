Many developers in Greece rarely invoice a Greek client: the money comes from Berlin, San Francisco, Toptal or Deel. Greece still expects a Greek setup. Here is what is specific to IT freelancers in 2026. The general basics are in our guide to [setting up as a freelancer in Greece](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece/).

## The short answer

- **Activity code (KAD):** usually **62.10.11.01** (software produced to order) under the [KAD 2025 list](https://www.aade.gr/allagi-antistoihisi-kad-me-toys-ishyontes-apo-132026), in force since 1 March 2026.
- **VAT:** 24% for Greek clients. **No Greek VAT** for EU business clients (reverse charge, VIES, monthly recapitulative statement) and for businesses outside the EU.
- **VAT returns:** **monthly** if you started with simple books on or after 1 January 2024; quarterly only on request after 24 months.
- **E-invoicing:** since 2 November 2026, invoices to Greek **and non-EU** business clients (US, UK, Switzerland) must be e-invoices.
- **US clients:** a W-8BEN, and normally no US withholding for work done in Greece.
- **EFKA:** from **€150.46/month** in the reduced class for new freelancers during their first five years.
- **Moving here?** [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/) exempts 50% of your income for seven years.

## Which KAD code for software development?

At your business start (έναρξη εργασιών) with AADE you pick a main activity code and secondary codes. Since 1 March 2026 these come from the new KAD 2025 list ([AADE decision A.1003/2026](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/2026-01/a1003fek.pdf)), based on the EU's NACE Rev. 2.1. The old 2008 codes **62.01** (programming) and **62.02** (IT consulting), still quoted in many blog posts, were mapped to the new list automatically for existing businesses in March 2026 ([A.1004/2026](https://www.aade.gr/allagi-antistoihisi-kad-me-toys-ishyontes-apo-132026)). Where AADE could not map a code centrally, the business had to file a change itself, so check your registration in myAADE.

| What you do | KAD 2025 |
|---|---|
| Custom software, backend, apps, contract development | 62.10.11.01 |
| Websites and web development | 62.10.11.04 |
| Software quality assurance and testing | 62.10.11.06 |
| Systems and software consulting (architecture, tech advisory) | 62.20.20 |
| Cybersecurity consulting | 62.20.50 |
| Technical support for computer systems | 62.20.30.03 |
| Web hosting | 63.10.12 |
| Licensing your own software | 58.29.41 |

</div>

Pick the code for what you invoice most and add secondary codes for the rest.

## Who is your client? Platforms change the answer

VAT and myDATA depend on **who you have the contract with**, not where the end user sits.

- **Direct clients:** you invoice the company itself.
- **Upwork:** your contract is with the client. Upwork's fee is a separate service it sells to you; with a valid VAT ID in your Upwork tax information, Upwork [does not collect VAT](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/31396875091219) from you.
- **Toptal:** talent work for clients under a [talent agreement with Toptal, LLC](https://www.toptal.com/talent-tpaa), a US company. If Toptal is the contracting party named in your agreement and pays you, Toptal is your client.
- **Deel as a contractor:** with a standard contractor contract, the client hires you and pays through Deel. Your contracting party is usually the client, and in Greece you issue your own invoice to it.
- **Deel Contractor of Record:** here Deel [signs the contract with you](https://www.deel.com/contractor-of-record) and pays you. The Deel entity named in your agreement is your client.

The legal name and country in your contract go on your [invoice](/guides/invoicing-foreign-clients-greece/).

## VAT on developer invoices

<div class="overflow-x-auto">

| Client | Greek VAT | Extra filings |
|---|---|---|
| Greek business | 24% | none |
| Business in another EU country | none, reverse charge | VIES registration, monthly recapitulative statement |
| Business outside the EU (US, UK, CH) | none | none |
| Consumers in other EU countries (apps, licences) | depends on volume | possibly OSS |

</div>

Recapitulative statements are due by the 26th of the following month; a late one costs €100. If you sell apps or licences to consumers in other EU countries, the €10,000 OSS threshold applies. Both are explained in [VAT for freelancers with foreign clients](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients/).

**How often you file:** under [AADE decision A.1049/2025](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/50029/a-1049-2025), freelancers with simple books who started on or after 1 January 2024 file **monthly**, by the last working day of the following month. After 24 months of activity they can switch to quarterly on request. Freelancers with simple books who started earlier file quarterly, unless they opt for monthly. A late return is fined automatically, even when it shows nothing to pay.

## myDATA and e-invoicing for developers

Every invoice is reported to [myDATA](/guides/mydata-greece-explained/), whatever the client's location: type **2.1** for Greek clients, **2.2** for EU business clients and **2.3** for clients outside the EU.

What changed in November 2026 is e-invoicing. Under [joint decision A.1197/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/55463/a-1197-2026), invoices to **Greek businesses and businesses outside the EU** must be issued as e-invoices from **2 November 2026**: through a certified provider or AADE's free [timologio](/guides/free-invoicing-greece-aade-timologio/) or myDATAapp. An invoice to a US startup, to Toptal or to a UK agency is in scope. Invoices to EU businesses are not. A transition period runs until **31 January 2027**, but only if you declared your invoicing method in time.

## US clients and the W-8BEN

US clients and platforms ask non-US contractors for a **Form W-8BEN**. Under the IRS rules, pay for services is [sourced where the work is performed](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-tege/withholding_and_reporting_of_income_tax_on_non-wage.pdf). Code you write in Greece is not US-source income, so no US withholding applies to it.

## Income tax and EFKA

Development profit is business income, taxed on the 2026 scale ([Law 5246/2025](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5246/2025/arthro/3)):

<div class="overflow-x-auto">

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| €0 – 10,000 | 9% |
| €10,000 – 20,000 | 20% |
| €20,000 – 30,000 | 26% |
| €30,000 – 40,000 | 34% |
| €40,000 – 60,000 | 39% |
| Over €60,000 | 44% |

</div>

Younger developers pay less on the first €20,000: 0% up to age 25, and 9% for ages 26 to 30; these rates also apply to business income. More on rates and the minimum presumed income in [freelancer taxes in Greece](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026/). Budget for the advance tax as well: 55% of this year's tax on business income is paid towards next year, halved in your first year with business income. Law 5355/2026, art. 12, cuts it to 50% from the return for tax year 2027, filed in 2028 (see [advance tax](/guides/hidden-costs-moving-to-greece-advance-tax/)).

Greek business clients withhold 20% income tax on invoices above €300, which you offset against your annual tax. Foreign clients and platforms don't withhold Greek tax.

**EFKA** is €250.77/month in the lowest regular class in 2026. New freelancers can apply for the special class of **€150.46/month** for their first five years ([e-EFKA circular 6/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/52023/egkyklios-e-efka-6-2026)); most add €10/month unemployment contribution. Details in [EFKA for freelancers](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece/).

## Deductible expenses for developers

Expenses with a proper invoice in your business's name reduce taxable profit: laptops, monitors and peripherals, software licences and subscriptions (IDEs, SaaS tools, AI assistants), cloud and hosting costs, domains, courses and conferences, and paid EFKA contributions. Under [Article 24 of the Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/24), an asset costing up to **€1,500** can, by choice, be written off in full in the year of purchase; more expensive computers and software are depreciated at 20% a year.

## Freelancer, EoR employee or IKE?

**Employed through Deel or another Employer of Record:** the EoR runs Greek payroll, withholds tax and pays EFKA. No VAT, no myDATA, but also no business deductions. See [working in Greece for a foreign employer](/guides/remote-employee-foreign-employer-greece/). If your only client is your former employer under the same conditions, a freelance setup can be challenged as disguised employment.

**IKE (private company):** 22% corporate tax plus 5% on dividends, about 25.9% on distributed profit. But double-entry books, monthly VAT returns and, in our experience, roughly twice the ongoing accounting cost of a sole proprietorship. IKE profits are also outside 5C. For most solo developers a sole proprietorship is simpler. Compare in [IKE setup and annual costs](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs/).

## Moving to Greece as a developer: Article 5C

[Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/) exempts **50% of employment or business income** from income tax for seven years. Developers qualify if they move their tax residence to Greece from an EU/EEA country or one with a tax cooperation agreement, were **not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years**, and either work for a Greek employer (or the Greek branch of a foreign company) or register as self-employed in Greece. Remote employees of a foreign company without a Greek presence don't qualify, and **Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible**. EFKA is not reduced. For EU citizens the usual routes are in [5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers/).
