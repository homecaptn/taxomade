Finding somewhere to live is usually the step that surprises people most about moving to Greece. The tax side is well documented and predictable; the rental market is informal, fast-moving and largely conducted in Greek. Knowing the mechanics in advance is what separates a smooth arrival from two months in an expensive short-let.

Here is how renting actually works, in the order you will encounter it.

## 1. You Need an AFM First

Before a lease can exist on paper, you need a Greek Tax Number. The landlord is obliged to declare your lease electronically to AADE, and your **AFM is the identifier AADE expects** for the tenant. Strictly speaking, [AADE's FAQ on lease declarations](https://www.forin.gr/articles/article/11099/suxnes-erwthseis-apanthseis-aade-misthwthria-akinhtwn) allows a foreign tenant without an AFM to be recorded by passport number — but in practice landlords and agents treat the AFM as a precondition, and you need your TAXISnet credentials (which come with the AFM) to accept the declaration from your side.

This creates a sequencing problem for new arrivals, and the practical answer is almost always the same: get the AFM first, bridge the gap with a short-term rental, then sign a long lease once the number is in hand. Our guide on [what an AFM is and how to get one](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number) covers that process — and it does not require a Greek address, so you can obtain one using a foreign address or a tax representative.

## 2. Budget for the Move-In Cost, Not the Rent

The advertised monthly rent is rarely what leaves your account in month one. Expect to pay:

- **First month's rent**, up front.
- **A deposit of one to two months.** Two is common in Athens. There is no legal cap.
- **An agency fee**, if a broker (*mesitis*) is involved — customarily one month's rent plus 24% VAT, paid by the tenant.

On a €700 apartment, that means about €1,400 on day one with a one-month deposit and no agent, and close to €3,000 with a two-month deposit and a broker. Greece also has **no state-run deposit protection scheme**. Your landlord holds the money personally, and recovering it depends largely on the relationship and on evidence. Photograph everything at handover and get a written condition note, however informal.

For orientation on what "normal" rent looks like: asking rents in central Athens averaged around €9.8 per square metre in 2026, Attica as a whole about €9.2 and Thessaloniki about €7.7, according to [RE/MAX transaction data](https://en.protothema.gr/2026/09/03/the-2026-rent-map-which-areas-saw-prices-soar-and-the-leaders-in-athens-thessaloniki-and-the-islands/) — so a 60 m² flat in the centre lands somewhere around €600, before extras.

## 3. What the Contract Should Say

Leases are frequently in Greek only. You are entitled to understand what you sign, so have it translated if there is any doubt — and check these points specifically:

- **Duration.** Greek law gives residential leases a **minimum three-year term**, even if the contract says one or two years ([article 2 of Law 1703/1987](https://www.odigostoupoliti.eu/pote-misthosi-katoikias-mporei-na-ischyei-gia-ligotero-apo-tria-eti/)). This cuts both ways: the landlord cannot end it early, but neither can you simply leave — unless the contract contains an early-termination clause or you agree a termination with the landlord. If you think you might move within the first year, negotiate that clause before signing.
- **Who pays the *koinochrista*.** These are the shared building charges — cleaning, lift, stairwell lighting, and in older blocks central heating. They are usually *not* included in the rent and can add a meaningful amount a month in winter.
- **Heating type.** Autonomous (you control it) versus central oil heating (you pay a share of the building's tank). The latter can be brutal in an Athens January.
- **Utility accounts.** Establish whether electricity and water move into your name or stay with the landlord, and confirm there are no outstanding balances attached to the property.
- **Rent by bank transfer.** Pay rent to the landlord's bank account from day one, even though the law making this mandatory ([article 210 of Law 5222/2025](https://taxrevenue.gr/ypoxreotiki-pliromi-enoikiou-trapeza/)) has been [postponed to 1 July 2027](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/55383/a-1187-2026). Cash leaves you with no proof of payment and, once the rule bites, no access to tenant subsidies.

Good news on costs: there is no stamp duty on residential leases. The 3.6% digital transaction fee that replaced stamp duty in 2025 applies to [commercial leases only](https://taxrevenue.gr/psifiako-telos-synallagis-epaggelmatikes-misthoseis/).

## 4. Registering the Lease with AADE

This is the step newcomers most often skip, and the one that matters most administratively.

Your landlord must submit a **Declaration of Lease Information** (*Δήλωση Πληροφοριακών Στοιχείων Μίσθωσης*) through myAADE [by the end of the month following the start of the tenancy](https://taxrevenue.gr/ilektronika-misthotiria/). You are then notified in myAADE and have **30 days to accept or reject** the declaration; if you do nothing, it counts as accepted ([AADE decision A.1068/2025](https://www.powergame.gr/forologia/943850/aade-prothesmia-30-imeron-gia-apodochi-misthotirion/)). Check the rent and the dates before you accept — acceptance cannot be reversed.

Do not treat this as paperwork between the landlord and the tax office. The registered lease is what gives you:

- **Proof of address** for a residence permit or [Digital Nomad Visa](/guides/ultimate-guide-dnv-greece) application.
- Supporting documentation when [opening a Greek bank account](/guides/open-bank-account-greece).
- A verifiable record if a deposit dispute ever arises.
- Access to the annual **rent refund** (*επιστροφή ενοικίου*): if you are a Greek tax resident, declare the rent in your tax return and meet the income limits (€25,000 single, €35,000 for couples plus €5,000 per child for the 2026 payment), AADE pays back one month's rent — capped at €800 — each November, [automatically and only for leases that match the electronic declaration](https://www.odigostoupoliti.eu/epistrofi-enoikiou-2026-pote-ginetai-pliromi-kai-poia-einai-ta-posa/).

A signed paper contract that was never declared is, for official purposes, close to worthless. Confirm the declaration has been filed before you hand over a deposit — or at the very least, before you need it.

## 5. Red Flags

The market moves quickly enough that scams find willing victims every season. Walk away from:

- **Any request to wire a deposit before viewing.** The "I'm currently abroad, I'll post the keys" message is the single most common fraud aimed at foreigners.
- **A landlord who refuses to register the lease.** This usually signals undeclared rental income — and it leaves you with no proof of address at all.
- **Rent quoted well below the area average.** Compare against our [cost of living breakdown](/guides/cost-of-living-greece-nomads) before assuming you have found a bargain.
- **Holiday lets dressed as long-term rentals.** A furnished flat offered at a suspiciously round monthly rate is often a short-let owner filling the winter, who will want it back in April.

## Summary

Secure your AFM first, budget two to four months' rent for move-in, read the contract for *koinochrista*, heating and an exit clause before you read it for the rent, pay by bank transfer, and treat the AADE lease registration as non-negotiable.
