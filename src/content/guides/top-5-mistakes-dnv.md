Applying for a visa in a foreign country involves navigating a maze of bureaucracy. The Greek Digital Nomad Visa (DNV) is an incredible opportunity, but the application process requires precision. A single missing stamp or misinterpreted requirement can lead to months of delays or an outright rejection.

Here are the top 5 most common mistakes applicants make—and how to avoid them.

## 1. Submitting Travel Insurance Instead of Private Health Insurance

This is by far the most frequent reason applications are delayed. 

Many applicants assume that standard nomad travel insurance (like SafetyWing or World Nomads) is sufficient. **It is not.** The Greek government requires a comprehensive **Private Health Insurance** policy from a provider that is officially authorized to operate in Greece. 

The policy must cover you for the entire duration of your visa and include specific coverage minimums for hospitalization and medical care within Greece. A policy designed just for travel emergencies will be rejected.

## 2. Misunderstanding the €3,500 Income Requirement

The financial threshold for the DNV is €3,500 per month. A critical mistake is assuming this refers to your gross salary. 

The requirement is strictly **€3,500 NET (after taxes)**. 

When the consulate reviews your application, they will look at your bank statements and payslips to verify that the actual cash hitting your account each month is at least €3,500. If your gross salary is €4,000 but your net take-home is only €3,200, your application will fail. Ensure your proof of income clearly demonstrates your net earnings.

## 3. Attempting to Apply Locally on a Tourist Visa

> [!WARNING]
> This mistake will now cost you your entire application.

Prior to February 2026, it was a common strategy to fly to Athens on a 90-day Schengen tourist visa and apply for the DNV from within the country to avoid dealing with embassies back home. 

**This is no longer legal.** Following the implementation of Law 5275/2026, all in-country conversions for the DNV were abolished. If you enter Greece as a tourist and attempt to apply, you will be rejected. You must apply for the Type-D Digital Nomad Visa at the Greek consulate in your home country (or country of legal residence) *before* you travel.

## 4. Missing Apostilles and Incorrect Translations

The Greek bureaucracy requires certainty that your foreign documents are genuine. Simply printing out a background check or marriage certificate is not enough.

Official documents (especially Criminal Record Certificates and civil registry documents) must bear an **Apostille stamp**—an international certification under the Hague Convention. 

Furthermore, these documents must be officially translated into Greek. The translation usually needs to be done by an authorized translator certified by the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs or a Greek lawyer. Using Google Translate or an uncertified online service will result in an immediate rejection.

## 5. Ignoring the 183-Day Tax Rule

Many nomads apply for the DNV focused entirely on the lifestyle and completely ignore the tax implications until it's too late.

If you use the DNV to live in Greece for more than 183 days in a calendar year, you will generally become a **Greek tax resident**. Failing to plan for this can lead to nasty surprises, including double taxation or fines for failing to declare global income in Greece.

Instead of ignoring it, proactive applicants use this rule to their advantage. By properly registering and applying for the **Article 5C (50% Tax Break)**, you can turn Greek tax residency into a massive financial benefit. But this requires planning *before* you hit the 183-day mark.

## Summary

The Greek DNV process is manageable, but it demands attention to detail. By securing the correct health insurance, proving your net income accurately, applying from your home country, getting your apostilles in order, and planning your tax strategy, you can ensure a smooth transition to your new life in the Mediterranean.
