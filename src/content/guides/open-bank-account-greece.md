While digital banks like Revolut and N26 are fantastic for daily life in Europe, moving to Greece long-term usually still means getting a traditional Greek bank account.

Why? Strictly speaking, Greek taxes can be paid from any SEPA account ([AADE publishes the IBANs and payment codes](https://www.aade.gr/sites/default/files/SEPA/SEPA_TAXISnet_EN.pdf)) and since September 2024 [refunds can go to a foreign SEPA IBAN](https://www.forin.gr/articles/article/80939/aade-dunatothta-pistwshs-epistrofwn-kai-katabolhs-poswn-se-iban-ekswterikou-entos-sepa). In practice, EFKA contributions, utility direct debits, IRIS payments and some payroll systems work cleanly only with a **Greek IBAN**. Refusing a foreign SEPA IBAN is illegal under [EU Regulation 260/2012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32012R0260), but you will still run into it.

Here is how to navigate the Greek banking system as a non-resident or new arrival.

## 1. The Pre-Requisite: You Must Have an AFM

Before you even approach a bank, get your [Greek Tax Number (AFM)](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number). Banks must record a tax number for every customer, and in practice no Greek bank will open an account for a newcomer without one.

Under the [Bank of Greece identification rules](https://www.lawspot.gr/voithitika-keimena/ti-ishyei-gia-ta-stoiheia-taytopoiisis-poy-zitoyn-oi-trapezes/) the bank must verify identity, address, phone number, profession and tax number, so bring:
- Your valid passport (EU citizens: national ID card also works).
- Proof of your home address, issued within the last six months (a utility bill from your home country, or your Greek rental contract as registered with AADE).
- Proof of income or profession (a recent payslip, employer letter, tax return, or business registration if self-employed).
- A phone number you can prove is yours (a phone bill). A Greek SIM is not a legal requirement, but many branches want one for the SMS codes in their apps.

Foreign documents may need an apostille and a translation into Greek or English.

## 2. The Four Major Greek Banks

Greece's banking sector is consolidated into four systemic banks. Here is a breakdown of their strengths for expats:

### [Piraeus Bank](https://www.piraeusbank.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Physical reach.
- **Why:** The [largest branch network in the country](https://www.hba.gr/4Statistika/UplPDFs/2025/%CE%94%CE%B9%CE%AC%CF%81%CE%B8%CF%81%CF%89%CF%83%CE%B7/2025-BranchNetworkPersonnelData.pdf) (368 branches at end-2025), including islands and rural areas, and no monthly fee on payment accounts.

### [Alpha Bank](https://www.alpha.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Opening an account before you arrive.
- **Why:** Alpha runs a [remote onboarding programme](https://www.alpha.gr/en/retail/alpha-bank-your-premier-banking-partner-in-greece/banking-with-us/opening-an-account-remotely) for people living in Germany, the UK, the USA, Cyprus or Israel: online form, document upload, a 30-minute video call, and account, e-banking and debit card are typically ready within about two business days. The myAlpha app is fully in English.

### [Eurobank](https://www.eurobank.gr/en)
- **Best for:** International customers who already have an AFM.
- **Why:** A separate [international customers unit](https://www.eurobank.gr/en/international-customers) with an English-speaking hotline seven days a week, remote account opening, and a published list of documents accepted from non-residents (foreign tax number, phone bill, utility bill).

### [National Bank of Greece (NBG)](https://www.nbg.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Tradition and stability.
- **Why:** The oldest bank in the country (founded 1841) with the second-largest network (305 branches). Bureaucracy can feel more rigid, and non-residents will usually need to visit a branch.

## 3. Can I Open an Account Remotely?

**Sometimes, and it depends less on your passport than on where you currently live.**

- **In-app onboarding is not for newcomers.** The "open an account from your phone" flows are built for people with a Greek ID card, Taxisnet login and Greek tax residency ([Piraeus](https://www.piraeusbank.gr/en/individuals/digital-solutions/digital-onboarding-service), [Alpha](https://www.alpha.gr/en/retail/myalpha/opening-an-account-online)). Do not count on them as a foreigner, EU or not.
- **Remote onboarding programmes:** Alpha's video-call route covers residents of Germany, the UK, the USA, Cyprus and Israel, with [more countries announced](https://greekreporter.com/2026/09/28/greek-bank-account-diaspora-us-uk-israel/). Eurobank's international unit also opens accounts remotely. Greek citizenship is not required; ties to Greece (an AFM, property, a planned move) are.
- **Power of Attorney:** Several banks let a representative in Greece, usually a lawyer, open the account on your behalf. Banks often insist on their own PoA wording, and you may still need one visit to sign.
- **Branch visit:** For everyone else, a branch appointment with the documents above. With a complete file it is done in one visit.

## 4. Banking Fees

Greek bank fees dropped sharply in 2025 after government intervention ([summary of the measures](https://www.tovima.com/finance/greece-implements-major-cuts-to-bank-fees-in-2025/)). As of 2026:
- **Account maintenance:** Alpha and NBG charge around €0.80/month, Eurobank €0.60/month, Piraeus nothing; usually waived if your salary is paid into the account or you hold €10,000 or more with the bank.
- **ATM withdrawals:** Since 11 August 2025 there is **no fee** at any Greek bank's ATM, whichever bank issued your card. Independent ATMs (Euronet and similar) are capped at **€1.50**.
- **Transfers:** Domestic transfers up to €5,000 cost at most €0.50 for the sender and €0.50 for the receiver; instant transfers cost the same as standard ones.
- **IRIS:** Instant phone-number payments between individuals are free, with limits of [€1,000 per day and €5,000 per month](https://www.tovima.com/society/greece-raises-iris-payment-limits-for-individuals-and-businesses) since January 2026. Businesses and freelancers have had to accept IRIS since 1 November 2025.
- Incoming international wire transfers from outside the SEPA zone may still incur receiving fees.

## 5. What About Neobanks?

Revolut gives Greek customers a Lithuanian IBAN, N26 a German one, Wise a Belgian one. All work for cards and most transfers, but not for IRIS, and occasionally get rejected by payroll or EFKA-related systems. Two things are changing: [Snappi](https://freenance.io/fintech/best-neobank-greece-2026-revolut-snappi-iban-eur-fees-foreigner-residency/), a Piraeus-backed digital bank licensed by the Bank of Greece, launched in 2025 with Greek IBANs, and Revolut [received approval for a Greek branch in July 2026](https://www.skai.gr/en/news/business/revolut-gets-bank-of-greece-approval-for-local-branch-plans-greek-iban-rollout), with Greek IBANs promised for "the coming months". Until then, a traditional bank is the safe choice for your tax and EFKA setup.

## Summary

For a smooth relocation, secure your AFM first, get a Greek SIM card (like Cosmote or Vodafone), and if you need an account before you arrive, start with Alpha's remote onboarding or Eurobank's international unit rather than hoping an app will let you in.
