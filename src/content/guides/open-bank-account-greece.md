While digital banks like Revolut and N26 are fantastic for daily life in Europe, moving to Greece long-term often requires a traditional Greek bank account. 

Why? Because the Greek state relies on a deeply integrated digital payment system (DIAS). Paying your EFKA (social security) contributions, settling tax bills, and sometimes even setting up direct debits for utilities require a **Greek IBAN**.

Here is how to navigate the Greek banking system as a non-resident or new arrival.

## 1. The Pre-Requisite: You Must Have an AFM

Before you even approach a bank, you must have your Greek Tax Number (AFM). No Greek bank will open an account for you without it. 

You will also need:
- Your valid Passport.
- Proof of your home address (a utility bill from your home country, or your Greek rental contract via TAXISnet).
- Proof of income or profession (a recent payslip, tax return, or employment contract).
- A Greek mobile phone number (for SMS verification and app setup).

## 2. The Four Major Greek Banks

Greece’s banking sector is consolidated into four major systemic banks. Here is a breakdown of their strengths for expats:

### [Piraeus Bank](https://www.piraeusbank.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Expats and remote workers.
- **Why:** Piraeus is widely regarded as the most expat-friendly bank. They have excellent English-speaking support, a robust 24-hour service line, and a strong branch presence not just in Athens, but across the islands and rural areas. They also frequently accept Power of Attorney for remote account opening.

### [Alpha Bank](https://www.alpha.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Digital experience.
- **Why:** Alpha Bank boasts the "myAlpha" app, which is consistently top-rated and fully localized in English. For individuals who already have an AFM and a Greek mobile number, Alpha Bank offers a very smooth online account opening process (though non-EU citizens may still face hurdles without a residence permit).

### [Eurobank](https://www.eurobank.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Remote setup options.
- **Why:** Eurobank has modernized significantly in recent years. They offer a great mobile app and are generally accommodating to remote account openings via Power of Attorney, making them a strong alternative to Piraeus.

### [National Bank of Greece (NBG)](https://www.nbg.gr/en)
- **Best for:** Extensive physical network.
- **Why:** NBG is the oldest and most traditional bank in the country. While their bureaucracy can feel a bit more rigid, they have the largest network of branches and ATMs nationwide.

## 3. Can I Open an Account Remotely?

**Yes, but it depends on your passport and representation.**

- **EU Citizens:** Can often utilize digital onboarding apps (like myAlpha) to open an account entirely from their smartphone, provided they have their AFM and Greek phone number ready.
- **Non-EU Citizens:** Digital onboarding is usually blocked for third-country nationals until they hold a physical Greek residence permit. 
- **The Solution (Power of Attorney):** If you are non-EU and need an account before you arrive (or before your permit is issued), you can authorize a Greek lawyer via Power of Attorney. Banks like **Piraeus** and **Eurobank** are particularly accommodating to lawyers opening accounts on behalf of foreign clients.

## 4. Banking Fees

Unlike many modern fintechs, traditional Greek banks still rely heavily on fees.
- Account maintenance is usually free or very low cost (under €2/month).
- **ATM Withdrawals:** Withdrawing cash from an out-of-network ATM (e.g., using a Piraeus card at an Alpha Bank machine) will incur a fee, typically between **€2.00 and €3.00** per transaction. 
- Incoming international wire transfers from outside the SEPA zone may also incur receiving fees.

## Summary

For a smooth relocation, secure your AFM first, get a Greek SIM card (like Cosmote or Vodafone), and prioritize Piraeus or Eurobank if you need to set up your financial life via a representative before you arrive.
