YouTube pays from Ireland, Twitch and Patreon from the US, and a Greek brand pays you directly. The tax rules for each of these are different, and Greece expects all of them in your books. Here is how creator income works in Greece in 2026, with the numbers we could verify.

## The short answer

- **Regular creator income is business income.** You register a sole proprietorship with AADE, keep books and pay EFKA.
- **YouTube (Google Ireland):** no Greek VAT, reverse charge. You need VIES registration and a monthly recapitulative statement.
- **US platforms (Twitch, Patreon and others):** no Greek VAT on business-to-business payouts from outside the EU.
- **Greek brand deals:** 24% VAT, unless you use the small business exemption (turnover up to €10,000).
- **US tax on YouTube:** 0% under the Greece–US treaty on Partner Program royalties, if you file a W-8BEN.
- **Income tax:** 9% to 44%. **EFKA:** from €150.46/month for new freelancers in their first five years.
- **Moving to Greece?** Article 5C can exempt 50% of your income for seven years.

## Is creator income business income?

Yes, as soon as you create content systematically to earn money. The tax authorities' position, confirmed in a [2026 dispute decision](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/54075/ded-1966-2026), is that systematic paid promotion is a business activity: register, keep books, issue invoices or receipts, pay EFKA. Smaller, occasional income still goes on your annual tax return.

In 2026 the tax authorities are actively checking. According to [Oikonomikos Tachydromos](https://www.ot.gr/2026/07/07/forologia/forologia-eidiseis/influencers-sarotikoi-elegxoi-gia-adilota-eisodimata-poies-einai-oi-ypoxreoseis-tous), audit teams monitor posts, hashtags and brand tags and cross-check them with bank accounts and contracts.

## Registering with AADE

You register a business activity (έναρξη εργασιών) for a sole proprietorship through [myAADE](https://www.aade.gr/en/node/11660). Three choices matter for creators:

- **Activity codes (KAD).** Since 1 March 2026, Greece uses the new [KAD 2025 list](https://www.aade.gr/allagi-antistoihisi-kad-me-toys-ishyontes-apo-132026), based on the EU's NACE Rev. 2.1. There is no single "influencer" code. You choose a main code and secondary codes that match what you actually sell, for example video production and advertising services.
- **VAT regime.** Normal VAT or the small business exemption (see below).
- **VIES registration.** Required before your first payout from Google Ireland or any other EU business.

Only if you hold an A1 certificate (the EU form confirming another country's social security covers you, e.g. during a temporary posting or when working in several EU countries) does EFKA not apply. Everyone else registers with EFKA as self-employed.

## VAT on platform payouts

VAT depends on who pays you, not where your viewers are. The details are in our guide to [VAT for freelancers with foreign clients](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients/).

### YouTube and AdSense: EU reverse charge

YouTube earnings are paid out through AdSense, and Google [states](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/142362) that all AdSense payments are made by Google Ireland and that the services are subject to the reverse charge under Article 196 of the EU VAT Directive. Google Ireland accounts for the VAT. You charge no Greek VAT, but you need:

- a VAT number registered in VIES,
- the income in your VAT return,
- a **recapitulative statement** for each month with a Google payout, due by the 26th of the following month. A late statement costs €100.

### Twitch, Patreon and other non-EU platforms

Patreon [says](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047578411-EU-Creator-Frequently-Asked-Tax-Questions) that creators contract with Patreon, Inc., a US company, and that Patreon charges and remits VAT on members' payments in its own name. For a business-to-business payout from outside the EU, your service is taxed where the client is: no Greek VAT, no reverse charge note. Twitch and other platforms work the same way if the paying entity is outside the EU. Check which company is named on your payout statement; that decides the treatment.

### Brand deals and sponsorships

A sponsorship with a Greek brand is a domestic sale: **24% VAT**. A brand in another EU country is invoiced under the reverse charge, a brand outside the EU without Greek VAT.

### The €10,000 small business exemption

If your turnover stays at or below **€10,000** in both the previous and the current year, you can opt for the [small business exemption](/guides/vat-small-business-exemption-greece/) and charge Greek brands no VAT. If you cross the limit, you switch to normal VAT immediately. With Google Ireland as a payer, the EU filings still apply.

## myDATA: every payout needs a document

Platforms pay automatically, but in Greece every business receipt is documented and transmitted to [myDATA](/guides/mydata-greece-explained/). For a monthly payout, that means an invoice from you to the paying company:

- **Google Ireland or another EU business:** myDATA type **2.2**.
- **US platforms and other non-EU businesses:** type **2.3**. Since 2 November 2026, invoices to non-EU businesses have to be [e-invoices](/guides/mydata-greece-explained/#e-invoicing-b2b-in-greece).
- **Greek brands:** type **2.1**, with VAT unless you are exempt.

Issue each invoice by the 15th of the month after the payout period at the latest. Details on fields and wording: [invoicing foreign clients from Greece](/guides/invoicing-foreign-clients-greece/).

Expect platforms to report you as well. Patreon, for example, [collects data from EU creators](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/21712170817293-Introduction-to-DAC7) under **DAC7**, the EU rules that make digital platforms report sellers' income to tax authorities.

## US tax: W-8BEN and YouTube

Google [withholds US tax](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362) only on the part of your earnings from US viewers, at 0–30% depending on your tax info. Without tax info submitted by 10 December, Google may deduct up to 24% of your **worldwide** earnings.

Submit a W-8BEN in AdSense with your Greek tax number as foreign TIN and claim the treaty. Google classes YouTube Partner Program income as "other copyright royalties". For Greece, the [IRS treaty table](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/tax-treaty-table-1.pdf) sets the rate on copyright royalties at **0%**. The form is valid for the year of submission plus three calendar years.

Twitch runs a similar tax interview through Amazon. Moving from the US? See our [US to Greece tax guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-us-tax-guide/).

## Income tax, EFKA and advance tax

Your profit is taxed like any [freelancer's](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026/):

| Taxable income (from 2026) | Rate |
|---|---|
| €0 – 10,000 | 9% |
| €10,000 – 20,000 | 20% |
| €20,000 – 30,000 | 26% |
| €30,000 – 40,000 | 34% |
| €40,000 – 60,000 | 39% |
| Over €60,000 | 44% |

- **Business levy:** the €650 trade fee was [abolished for individuals](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5162/2024/arthro/3) from tax year 2024.
- **EFKA:** €250.77/month in the lowest regular class in 2026, or **€150.46/month** in the special class for new freelancers during their first five years, on application. Most add €10/month unemployment contribution. More in [EFKA for freelancers](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece/).
- **Advance tax:** with your return you pay 55% of this year's tax on business income towards next year, halved in your first year. It drops to 50% from the return for tax year 2027. See [hidden costs of moving to Greece](/guides/hidden-costs-moving-to-greece-advance-tax/).

## Gifts, donations and merch

- **Gifted products and trips:** if a brand gives you something in exchange for promotion, the tax authorities treat it as payment. A 2026 decision of AADE's Dispute Resolution Directorate ([ΔΕΔ 1966/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/circulars/54075/ded-1966-2026)) treats such barter as payment that has to be invoiced and declared.
- **Memberships, Super Chats, Bits and subscriptions:** they arrive as part of the platform payout and are business income.
- **Merch:** selling goods is a separate activity with its own KAD and VAT rules. Add it to your registration before you sell.

## Equipment and other deductions

Business expenses with a proper invoice reduce your taxable profit: cameras, microphones, lighting, editing software, studio rent, ads, and the business share of phone and internet. Under [Article 24 of the Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/24), an asset costing up to **€1,500** can be written off in full in the year you buy it. More expensive computers and software are depreciated at 20% a year, other equipment over several years.

## Moving to Greece as a creator: Article 5C

If you move your tax residence to Greece, [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/) exempts **50% of your income** from income tax for seven years. Conditions:

- not Greek tax resident in **5 of the previous 6 years**,
- moving from an EU/EEA country or one with a tax cooperation agreement with Greece,
- the income comes from your own **Greek sole proprietorship**. Profits from an IKE are not covered,
- you stay at least two years.

**Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible**: the visa doesn't allow Greek business activity. See [5C vs Digital Nomad Visa](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa/). EFKA is not reduced.
