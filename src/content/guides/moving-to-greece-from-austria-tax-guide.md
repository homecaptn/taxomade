Austrians moving to Greece are mostly freelancers with Austrian clients, remote employees, GmbH owners and retirees. The Greek side is the same for everyone; what differs is the Austrian exit. This guide covers that exit and points you to the right Greek setup.

## The short answer

- **Deregister** within three days of moving out, but a flat you keep can still make you Austrian tax resident.
- **The year you leave** is assessed in Austria only for the months you were resident; employees often get a refund.
- **Exit tax** hits shares, funds and crypto bought as new assets; for a move to Greece you can defer it until you sell.
- **The 2007 treaty:** freelance income and most pensions are taxed in Greece; Austrian rent and civil service pensions stay Austrian.
- **Social security follows your work:** freelancers working from Greece join EFKA.
- **Article 5C** (50% exemption for 7 years) and **Article 5B** (7% on foreign pensions for 15 years) are open to Austrian movers who qualify.

## 1. Ending Austrian tax residency

Under §1 EStG, you are fully taxable in Austria while you have a **residence (Wohnsitz) or habitual abode** there. You [deregister your main residence](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/themen/eu_und_international/auslandsoesterreicher/Seite.480104.html) within three days of giving it up; naming Greece as the destination is enough.

The Meldezettel is not the tax test, though. A flat that stays available to you can keep you fully taxable. The second-home relief only helps people whose centre of life has been abroad for more than five years and who use the Austrian flat on no more than 70 days a year.

If both countries treat you as resident, the [Austria–Greece treaty](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXIII/I/264/imfname_089416.pdf) (signed in Athens on 18 July 2007, BGBl. III Nr. 16/2009) decides: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode, then nationality. In Greece, you become [tax resident](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days) with your main home or centre of life here, or after 183 days in 12 months.

## 2. The year you leave

When you move during the year, Austria makes [two separate assessments](https://findok.bmf.gv.at/findok/resources/pdf/947dadb5-daf5-453b-b46c-781d3a39f5b2/73832.1.X.X.pdf): one for the resident period, covering the income you received then, and one for Austrian income afterwards. Employees had wage tax withheld as if they would earn all year, so the **Arbeitnehmerveranlagung** for that year often brings money back. You can file it through FinanzOnline up to five years later.

From the next year, Austria only taxes Austrian-source income, such as rent. Tell your Austrian bank you now live in Greece; the account is reported to Greece under the automatic exchange of information.

## 3. Exit tax on shares, funds and crypto

This is what most Austrian movers miss. Under [§27 (6) no. 1 EStG](https://www.ris.bka.gv.at/eli/bgbl/1988/400/P27/NOR40274825), anything that restricts Austria's right to tax a capital asset counts as a sale: shares, fund units, derivatives and cryptocurrencies. Moving to Greece does exactly that, because the treaty gives future gains to Greece.

The gain up to your departure is taxed at **27.5%**. Only new assets are caught: shares and funds bought from 2011, crypto from March 2021.

For a move within the EU/EEA there is relief:

- **Private assets:** on application, the tax is assessed but [not collected until you actually sell](https://www.wko.at/steuern/besteuerung-kapitalvermoegen). A later move outside the EU/EEA counts as a sale.
- **Business assets:** the exit tax is paid in instalments, over five years for fixed assets and two for current assets.

GmbH shares are caught too. If you run the GmbH alone from Greece, its place of management, and with it its tax residence, can move as well. Talk to your Steuerberater before you deregister. For a new Greek company, see [setting up an IKE](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs).

## 4. The double tax treaty

| Income | Taxed in |
|---|---|
| Freelance work done from Greece | Greece, unless you keep a fixed base in Austria |
| Employment | Where the work is physically done |
| Rent from Austrian property | Austria; Greece credits |
| Statutory (PVA, SVS), company and private pensions | Greece only |
| Civil service pensions | Austria, unless you are a Greek national living in Greece |
| Austrian dividends | Austria may keep 15% (27.5% withheld, the rest reclaimable); Greece credits |
| Gains on shares after the move | Greece |

## 5. Freelancers and remote workers

You close or suspend your Austrian Gewerbe (or inform the Finanzamt and SVS as a Neuer Selbständiger), register as a sole trader in Greece and invoice from there.

- **Austrian business clients** are invoiced without VAT under the reverse charge; the Austrian small-business exemption no longer applies ([VAT with foreign clients](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients)).
- **Social security:** under EU Regulation 883/2004 you are insured where you work, so with EFKA, from about €150 per month for new freelancers ([EFKA guide](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece)). An A1 only covers temporary postings.
- **Greek routine:** VAT returns monthly for the first 24 months for new businesses with simple books, then quarterly on request, myDATA for every invoice, B2B e-invoicing mandatory from 2 November 2026 with a transition to 31 January 2027 ([e-invoicing](/guides/e-invoicing-greece-explained)), and a [tax advance](/guides/hidden-costs-moving-to-greece-advance-tax) halved only in your first year with business income.

Steps in order: [AFM](/guides/what-is-greek-afm-number), [freelancer setup](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece), [freelancer taxes 2026](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026).

**Employees** who keep an Austrian contract and work permanently from Greece are taxed in Greece on those workdays and fall under EFKA, so the employer must register in Greece. Many switch to freelancing instead ([5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers)).

## 6. Retirees

- **Tax:** statutory and private pensions are taxed in Greece. The PVA keeps withholding Austrian tax until you send a [Greek residence certificate on form ZS-QU1](https://pv.at/de/flipbooks/PV-504/flipbook.pdf). Once a year you return a proof of life.
- **Civil servants:** a Beamtenpension stays taxable in Austria; you declare it in Greece, which credits the Austrian tax.
- **Health:** with only an Austrian pension, you stay insured through Austria and get Greek care via form S1; a health contribution is deducted from the pension.
- **Article 5B:** if you were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years, your foreign income is taxed at 7% for 15 years. Apply by 31 October (A.1192/2026). Whether it beats the normal 9–44% scale depends on your pension ([7% flat tax](/guides/retiring-to-greece-7-percent-flat-tax)).

Familienbeihilfe generally ends once the family lives in Greece and no parent works or draws a pension in Austria. Austria's indexation of benefits for children abroad was [ruled contrary to EU law](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62020CJ0328) in 2022 (C-328/20). Austrian pension periods are kept and combined with Greek ones under EU rules.

## 7. Article 5C: the 50% exemption

If you were not Greek tax resident in **5 of the previous 6 years**, [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) exempts 50% of Greek employment or business income for **7 years**. You need a Greek employer, a Greek establishment of a foreign employer, or your own business in Greece. Start by 2 July and you apply by 31 December of that year; later, by 31 December of the next. AADE allows it alongside 5A or 5B. **Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible** ([5C vs DNV](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa)).

## Frequently asked questions

### When does my Austrian tax liability end if I move to Greece?

When you no longer have a residence or habitual abode in Austria. Deregistering is the first step, but a flat you keep available can still count as a residence. If both countries claim you, the 2007 treaty decides: permanent home first, then centre of vital interests.

### Does Austria charge exit tax when I move to Greece?

Yes, on shares and funds bought from 2011 and crypto bought from March 2021, which Austria treats as sold when you leave. For a move to Greece, you can apply to have the tax assessed but not collected until you actually sell.

### Is my Austrian pension taxed in Greece or in Austria?

Statutory pensions (PVA, SVS) and private or company pensions are taxed only in Greece once you live there. Civil service pensions stay taxable in Austria unless you are a Greek national living in Greece. To stop Austrian withholding, you give the PVA a Greek residence certificate on form ZS-QU1.

### Do I keep paying SVS contributions if I freelance from Greece?

No. Under EU Regulation 883/2004 you are insured where you work, so a freelancer living and working in Greece joins EFKA. An A1 certificate only covers temporary postings, not a permanent move.

### Can I use the Greek 50% tax break (Article 5C) when moving from Austria?

Yes, if you were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years and take a job with a Greek employer or register your own business in Greece. Digital Nomad Visa holders are not eligible.

*Rules as of October 2026 (EStG §§1 and 27, Austria–Greece tax treaty 2007, EU Regulation 883/2004, Greek Income Tax Code art. 5B/5C, A.1192/2026).*
