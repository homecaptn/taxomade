With Greece becoming a hub for remote workers, a common question is: *"Can't I just work from Greece on my tourist visa?"* For a short holiday, the short stay is what you have. For anything longer, the legal and tax side looks very different. This guide compares the Schengen short stay with the Greece Digital Nomad Visa (DNV).

## The short answer

- **Short stay:** up to 90 days in any 180, visa-free for many passports, no right to work in Greece.
- **DNV:** a national visa from the Greek consulate before you travel, then a two-year renewable residence permit.
- **Income for the DNV:** €3,500 a month net (+20% spouse, +15% per child).
- **Tax:** more than 183 days within 12 months usually makes you Greek tax resident on your worldwide income.
- **No 5C:** the DNV does not give access to the 50% tax break.

## 1. The Schengen short stay (90/180 rule)

Citizens of countries such as the US, UK, Canada and Australia need no visa for a short stay: you can spend up to **90 days within any 180-day period** in the Schengen Area. The EU's planned [ETIAS travel authorisation](https://travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en) (€20, valid three years) will add an online pre-check before travel once it starts. It is not a visa and does not change the 90-day limit; check the official site for the start date. The Entry/Exit System (EES) already records entries and exits digitally, so the 90/180 count is checked automatically.

The limits:

- **No work authorisation:** a short stay is for tourism, visits and short business trips. It gives you no right to work in Greece, and long-term remote work from Greece on a short stay is at best a legal grey area, not something to build a move on.
- **Time limits:** the 180-day window is rolling. Once you have used 90 days, you cannot re-enter until enough earlier days have dropped out of the window. Overstaying triggers a fine on departure under [article 20 of the Migration Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5038/2023/arthro/20) and can affect future applications.

### No more switching inside Greece

In the past, many nomads entered as tourists and applied for the digital nomad residence permit while already in Greece. **Since February 2026, this route is no longer available in practice.** Under [Law 5275/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5275/2026) (Government Gazette A' 17, 6 February 2026), a national Type-D visa from a Greek consulate is the first step: you apply from your country of residence, before you arrive. Confirm the current procedure with your consulate. Overstaying in the hope of applying locally leads to fines, not a permit.

## 2. The Greece Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)

The DNV is for non-EU citizens who want to live in Greece while working remotely for employers or clients based abroad. The rules sit in [article 68 of the Migration Code (Law 5038/2023)](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5038/2023/arthro/68): a stable income of at least **€3,500 net per month** (plus 20% for a spouse or partner and 15% per child), work for employers or clients outside Greece, a declaration that you will not provide work or services to any employer based in Greece, and a €75 visa fee at the consulate. The full process is in our [Digital Nomad Visa guide](/guides/ultimate-guide-dnv-greece).

What it gives you:

1. **Legal residence:** a national visa valid for up to 12 months, followed by a two-year renewable residence permit (type I.8) that you apply for in Greece before the visa expires; the permit fee is €1,000.
2. **Compliance:** your stay matches what you actually do.
3. **Schengen travel:** time in Greece on a Greek visa or residence permit does not count against the 90/180 rule. You can still visit other Schengen states for up to 90 days in any 180-day period ([Schengen Convention, art. 21](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:42000A0922(02))).

**What it doesn't allow:** employment with a Greek employer or business activity in Greece; family members on your permit may not work here either. This matters for the tax question below.

## 3. The 183-day rule and tax

A common misconception is that if your employer is in the US or UK, you only owe tax there. That is not how it works.

Greek tax residency is governed by [article 4 of the Income Tax Code](https://aade.gr/omogeneis-katoikoi-exoterikoy/eisodima/forologiki-katoikia-gia-fysika-prosopa-fke). If you are present in Greece for more than **183 days, cumulatively, within any 12-month period**, you become a **Greek tax resident** from your first day of presence and are taxed here on your worldwide income. You can also become resident earlier if Greece becomes your centre of vital interests: home, family and economic ties. Our guide on the [183-day rule](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days) walks through the counting.

- **Short stay:** a stay within the 90-day limit normally does not make you Greek tax resident. Greek law also excludes stays purely for tourism or medical reasons from the 183-day count, but working while you are here is not a purely tourist purpose.
- **Digital Nomad Visa:** the DNV is built for living here. Unless you spend close to half the year elsewhere, you will cross 183 days and file a Greek return on your worldwide income. Pay for work physically done in Greece is usually taxed here first; if another country also taxes it, the double tax treaty (where one exists) decides how relief is given.

### What the DNV does not give you: the 50% tax break

You will read elsewhere that nomads can combine the DNV with Greece's **Article 5C** incentive, which exempts 50% of Greek employment or business income from income tax for seven years. They cannot. [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) requires employment with a Greek legal entity (or a Greek branch of a foreign company) or an individual business activity in Greece, on top of not having been Greek tax resident in five of the previous six years. The DNV rules out exactly that. As a DNV holder you pay the normal progressive rates (9% to 44%) once resident. More in [why the DNV rules out the 50% tax break](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa).

## Summary

For a short summer stay, a visa-free visit (if your passport allows it) is the normal option for a holiday, but it does not give you a right to work from Greece.

If you want to relocate, rent long-term and build a life here, the **Digital Nomad Visa**, applied for at a consulate before you arrive, is the route built for exactly this: living in Greece while working for employers or clients abroad. Go in with clear eyes on tax: it usually makes you a Greek tax resident, and it does not unlock the 50% break.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work remotely from Greece on a tourist visa?

A visa-free short stay is not a work authorisation. Short remote work is a grey area at best; for a longer stay, the Digital Nomad Visa is the route built for non-EU citizens working for employers or clients abroad.

### Can I switch from a tourist stay to the Digital Nomad Visa inside Greece?

Not in practice since Law 5275/2026: you apply for the Type-D visa at a Greek consulate in your country of residence before you travel. Confirm the current procedure with your consulate.

### Does time on a Greek Digital Nomad Visa count toward the Schengen 90/180 rule?

No. Time in Greece on a Greek national visa or residence permit doesn't count; you can visit other Schengen states for up to 90 days in any 180.

### Do I become a Greek tax resident on the Digital Nomad Visa?

Usually yes: after more than 183 days within any 12 months, or when your centre of life moves to Greece. You then file a Greek return on your worldwide income.

### Can Digital Nomad Visa holders use the 50% tax break (Article 5C)?

No. The DNV does not allow work for Greek employers or clients or business activity in Greece, which is exactly what Article 5C requires.
