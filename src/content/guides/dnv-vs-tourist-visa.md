With Greece becoming a global hub for remote workers, a common question arises: *"Can't I just work from Greece on my tourist visa?"* 

While the temptation to simply pack a laptop and head to the islands for a few months is high, the legal and tax implications are significant. In this guide, we break down the differences between a Schengen short stay and the official Greece Digital Nomad Visa (DNV).

## 1. The Schengen Short Stay (90/180 Rule)

For citizens of countries like the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, entering Greece for tourism is easy: no visa is needed, and you can stay in the Schengen Area for up to **90 days within any 180-day period**. From the last quarter of 2026 the EU plans to require visa-exempt travellers to hold an [ETIAS travel authorisation](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/united-states-america/travelling-europe-etias_en) (€20, valid three years) before boarding — an online pre-check, not a visa, and it does not change the 90-day limit.

However, there are strict limitations:
- **No Legal Right to Work:** A short stay strictly prohibits remunerated activity in Greece. While casually checking emails is rarely policed, technically, working a full-time remote job on a tourist stay violates immigration law.
- **Time Limits:** The 180-day window is rolling. Once you have used 90 days, you cannot re-enter until enough earlier days have dropped out of the window — in the simplest case, 90 days outside Schengen after a full 90-day stay. Overstaying triggers a fine on departure under [article 20 of the Migration Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5038/2023/arthro/20) and jeopardises future visa applications.

### The End of In-Country Conversions
> [!WARNING]
> In the past, many nomads entered Greece as tourists and applied for the Digital Nomad residence permit while already inside the country. **As of February 2026, this is no longer possible.** Under [Law 5275/2026](https://www.zeya.com/newsletters/new-immigration-law-52752026-key-provisions-and-practical-implications) (Government Gazette A' 17, 6 February 2026), a national type-D visa from a Greek consulate is a mandatory first step — you apply from your country of residence, before you arrive. Overstaying your tourist stay in the hope of applying locally will result in fines and no permit.

## 2. The Greece Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)

The Digital Nomad Visa is designed specifically for non-EU citizens who wish to live in Greece while working remotely for employers or clients based abroad. The rules sit in [article 68 of the Migration Code (Law 5038/2023)](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5038/2023/arthro/68): you need a stable income of at least **€3,500 net per month** (plus 20% for a spouse or partner and 15% per child), you declare that you will not provide work or services to any employer based in Greece, and you pay a €75 visa fee at the consulate. 

### Key Advantages of the DNV:
1. **Legal Residency:** It grants you the legal right to reside in Greece for a long-term period (a 12-month national visa, followed by a 2-year renewable residence permit applied for in Greece, with a €1,000 permit fee).
2. **Peace of Mind:** You are compliant with local immigration laws.
3. **Schengen Travel:** Time spent in Greece on a Greek visa or residence permit does not count against the 90/180 rule. You can still visit other Schengen states for [up to 90 days in any 180-day period](https://www2.mfa.gov.lv/en/consular-information/news/10497-travel-with-residence-permits-and-long-stay-visas-issued-by-the-schengen-states), as a resident-permit holder rather than a tourist.
4. **No Right to Work in Greece:** The flip side of the permit — it does not allow employment with a Greek employer or business activity in Greece. This matters for the tax question below.

## 3. The 183-Day Rule and Tax Implications

One of the biggest misconceptions about remote work is that if your employer is in the US or UK, you only owe taxes there. This is false.

Greek tax residency is governed by [article 4 of the Income Tax Code](https://aade.gr/omogeneis-katoikoi-exoterikoy/eisodima/forologiki-katoikia-gia-fysika-prosopa-fke). If you are present in Greece for more than **183 days, cumulatively, within any 12-month period** — not just a calendar year — you become a **Greek tax resident** from your first day of presence, and are taxed in Greece on your worldwide income. You can also become resident earlier if Greece becomes your "centre of vital interests" — where your home, family and economic ties are. Our guide on the [183-day rule](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days) walks through the counting. 

- **Tourist Stay:** If you stay under 90 days, you usually avoid triggering Greek tax residency.
- **Digital Nomad Visa:** The DNV is built for living here. Unless you spend close to half the year elsewhere, you will cross 183 days — and you must prepare to navigate Greek tax obligations: a tax return on worldwide income, with relief under the applicable double tax treaty for tax already paid abroad.

### What the DNV Does Not Give You: The 50% Tax Break

You will read elsewhere that nomads can "combine" the DNV with Greece's **Article 5C** incentive, which exempts 50% of income from Greek income tax for seven years. They cannot. [Article 5C](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/5Γ) requires employment with a Greek legal entity (or a Greek branch of a foreign company) or an individual business activity in Greece, on top of not having been Greek tax resident in five of the previous six years. The DNV forbids exactly that Greek employment and business activity. As a DNV holder you therefore pay standard progressive Greek income tax (9% to 44%) on your worldwide income once resident, with treaty relief for tax paid abroad. We explain the clash in [why the DNV disqualifies you from the 50% tax break](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa) and the incentive itself in our [Article 5C guide](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c).

The practical takeaway: the DNV solves your immigration status, not your tax bill. Budget for Greek tax from year one and get your filing position clear before you pass 183 days — we prepare and file the Greek returns for DNV holders, and a compliance check before you move is the cheapest point to do it.

## Summary

If your goal is a short 2-month summer working holiday, a visa-free short stay (if your passport allows it) might be the practical reality for many, despite the technical grey area of remote work. 

However, if you want to truly relocate, rent a long-term apartment and integrate into the community, the **Digital Nomad Visa** — applied for at a consulate before you arrive — is the only legal, sustainable route. Just go in with clear eyes on tax: it makes you a Greek tax resident, and it does not unlock the 50% break.
