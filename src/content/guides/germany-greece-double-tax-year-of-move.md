The year you move from Germany to Greece is when double taxation actually happens: not because the treaty fails, but because the two countries draw the line between "before" and "after" differently. This guide covers that year. The treaty article by article is in our [Germany–Greece tax treaty guide](/guides/germany-greece-double-tax-treaty/); deregistration, social security and health insurance are in the [Germany to Greece moving guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide/).

## The short answer

- **Germany taxes you until you leave.** One German return for the year covers the months before your move plus German-source income afterwards. Your Greek earnings aren't taxed in Germany, but they raise your German rate (Greek rent does not).
- **Greece taxes you from your first day here**, provided you stay more than 183 days in any 12-month period. From that day, Greece taxes worldwide income.
- **The danger zone is overlap:** a German flat you keep, invoices for German work paid after the move, bonuses, German rent, and GmbH shares.
- **Make the dates match:** your German deregistration, your arrival in Greece and the start of your Greek registration should tell the same story.
- **Keep the paperwork:** German assessment, wage tax statements and a Greek certificate of tax residence.

## How each country splits the year

### Germany: one return, two periods

German income tax is annual, but residence can end mid-year. Under [§2(7) EStG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/EStG/2.h.html), German-source income from the months after you leave is included in that one resident assessment. Your Greek income for the rest of the year isn't taxed in Germany, but under [§32b(1) no. 2 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__32b.html) it counts towards the rate on your German income (Progressionsvorbehalt). Rent from Greek property is different: while you are German resident, the treaty exempts it and, as EU property income, it does not raise your rate ([§ 32b(1) sentence 2 no. 3 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__32b.html)).

### Greece: resident from day one

Under the Greek Income Tax Code, an individual present in Greece for more than 183 days in any 12-month period is tax resident [from the first day of presence](https://aade.gr/en/greeks-abroad-non-residents/income-taxation/tax-residence-natural-persons-itc). Holiday or medical stays up to 365 days don't count. Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents only on Greek-source income. Details: [tax residency in Greece](/guides/tax-residency-greece-183-days/).

Greek law has no formal split-year rule. But because residence starts on your first day of presence, German salary or fees earned before you arrived generally fall into your non-resident period and stay out of Greek tax. Your first Greek return ([form E1](/guides/greek-tax-return-e1-foreigners/), filed from mid-March to 15 July of the next year) should reflect the date you became resident, so keep proof of it: flight, lease, registration.

### A worked example

Julia, a freelance UX designer, lives in Munich until 30 June 2026 and arrives in Athens on 1 July. She deregisters in Munich, gives up her flat, and registers her Greek sole proprietorship in July.

- **Germany, 2026 return:** taxes her German freelance profit for January to June. Her Greek profit from July to December is listed but only used to set the rate.
- **Greece, 2026 return (filed in 2027):** taxes her Greek business income from 1 July. January to June isn't in it.
- **Result:** each euro is taxed once. The progression effect in Germany is a higher rate, not double tax.

## Five overlap traps

### 1. A German home you keep

Under [§8 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__8.html), you keep a German domicile as long as you have a home there that you can use. Deregistering alone doesn't end it; the flat decides. Then both countries see you as resident, and Article II of the treaty breaks the tie: [permanent home first, then centre of vital interests](https://datenbank.nwb.de/Dokument/94976_II/), then habitual abode, then nationality.

With a home in both countries, the centre of vital interests decides: where your partner and children live, where you work, where your social life is. That is an argument, not a certainty. Selling, renting out long-term or giving up the flat removes it.

Same problem in reverse: if you arrive in Greece on 1 May but only deregister on 30 June, May and June are a dual-residence period.

### 2. German work, paid after the move

Freelancers: finish and invoice everything you did in Germany from your German business, under your German tax number, before or at the move. Start Greek invoices only once your Greek business is registered and reporting to [myDATA](/guides/mydata-greece-explained/). Germany treats fees for work done while you lived there as German income, while Greece taxes what your Greek business invoices. A July invoice for May's project, issued from the Greek business, can put the same income in two returns.

### 3. Bonuses and back pay

For treaty purposes, Germany allocates bonuses to the period they were earned for, not the date they are paid ([BMF letter of 12 December 2023](https://www.steuerschroeder.de/blog/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/2023-12-12-steuerliche-behandlung-arbeitslohn-doppelbesteuerungsabkommen.pdf), as amended on [19 December 2025](https://www.steuerschroeder.de/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-12-19-steuerliche-behandlung-des-arbeitslohns.pdf)). Your 2026 bonus for German work, paid in March 2027 while you live in Athens, is pay for work performed in Germany. Under Article XI(2) Germany may tax it, and the 183-day exception in Article XI(3) doesn't help because your employer is German.

If Greece also taxes it as income of a resident, it credits the German tax, but only up to the Greek tax on that income. If Germany withheld more, the difference is lost.

For salary you earn while working from Greece, the employer can usually stop German wage tax on Greek workdays with an exemption certificate from its tax office (Betriebsstättenfinanzamt, §39(4) no. 5 EStG). Apply before the first salary after the move.

### 4. German rent and investments

Rent from a German flat stays taxable in Germany all year (Article XIII). In Greece it enters your return only for the months you were resident; Greece credits the German tax on that share, up to the Greek tax on it.

German dividends and interest work the same way; rates and refunds are in the [treaty guide](/guides/germany-greece-double-tax-treaty/).

### 5. GmbH shares and the exit tax

The German exit tax under [§6 AStG](https://lxgesetze.de/astg/6) is triggered on the day your unlimited liability ends. If you hold **at least 1%** of a corporation and were taxable in Germany for **at least 7 of the last 12 years**, the gain on your shares is taxed as if you had sold them that day, in your move-year return. Since 2022 there is no open-ended deferral for EU moves; the timing points are:

- **Valuation date:** the day your unlimited liability ends, usually when you give up your German home. That is why the deregistration date matters.
- **Payment:** on application, seven annual instalments, usually against security. The first is due a month after the assessment, the rest on 31 July of each following year.
- **Reporting:** while instalments run, you confirm your address and shareholding to the Finanzamt every year by 31 July.

The return-within-seven-years rule is in the [Germany to Greece moving guide](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide/). Plan this before the move; once you've left, the valuation date is fixed.

## The year after: extended limited liability

[§2 AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__2.html) can keep Germany taxing more of your German income for the rest of the move year and the **ten years after it**. It applies to German citizens who were taxable in Germany for at least five of the last ten years, move to a low-tax country and keep substantial economic interests in Germany (for example, a qualifying GmbH stake, or German income above 30% of the total or €62,000). The treaty still limits what Germany can actually tax.

Greece's normal rates aren't low under the German test. But a preferential regime can count, such as the 50% exemption under [Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/) or the 7% regime for pensioners under Article 5B. The rule only applies if the German income covered exceeds **€16,500** a year, and you escape it if your total tax reaches at least two-thirds of what Germany would charge. If you plan 5C or 5B and keep significant German income, have a German adviser check this before the move.

## Paperwork that settles disputes

1. **German deregistration certificate** (Abmeldebescheinigung), dated to match your arrival in Greece.
2. **Greek registration** as a resident with your Greek address in the tax register, plus your business or employment start.
3. **German certificate of residence** for earlier years from your Finanzamt. AADE may ask for it in a 5C application to confirm you weren't Greek resident before.
4. **Greek certificate of tax residence**, issued online by AADE in Greek and English for the current year or earlier years you filed as a resident (see [how to get the Greek tax residency certificate](/guides/greek-tax-residency-certificate/)). German banks and tax offices may ask for it.
5. **German tax assessment and wage tax statement** for the move year. These are your proof for every Greek credit.

If both countries still tax the same income, Article XX lets you ask your residence country's tax authority to settle it with the other.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I pay tax in Germany and Greece in the year I move?

You file in both, but each country taxes a different part of the year. Germany taxes your income up to the date you leave, and your Greek earnings afterwards only raise the German rate. Greece taxes your worldwide income from your first day of residence and credits German tax on German-source income from that period, up to the Greek tax on it.

### When do I become tax resident in Greece after moving from Germany?

If you stay more than 183 days in any 12-month period, Greece treats you as resident from your first day of presence. A permanent home or centre of vital interests in Greece can make you resident even with fewer days. Germany's unlimited tax liability ends when you give up your German home and habitual abode.

### What if I keep my flat in Germany after moving to Greece?

A home that stays available to you can keep you resident in Germany under §8 AO, so both countries may treat you as resident. The 1966 treaty's tie-breaker then decides, starting with permanent home and then centre of vital interests. Selling, renting out long-term or giving up the flat avoids the question.

### Who taxes a bonus paid after I moved to Greece?

Germany allocates salary to the period in which it was earned. A bonus for work done in Germany can be taxed in Germany even if it is paid after you moved, because your employer is German. If Greece also taxes it, it credits the German tax, up to the Greek tax on that income.

### When is the German exit tax triggered if I move to Greece?

On the day your unlimited German tax liability ends, usually the day you give up your German home. If you hold at least 1% of a corporation and were taxable in Germany for at least 7 of the last 12 years, the gain on the shares is taxed as if you had sold them that day. Since 2022, EU moves get no open-ended deferral, only seven annual instalments on application.
