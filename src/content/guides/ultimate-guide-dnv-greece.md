Greece has rapidly become one of Europe’s top destinations for remote workers. With a Mediterranean climate, rich culture, and an excellent quality of life, it's easy to see why. For non-EU/EEA and non-Swiss citizens, the **Greece Digital Nomad Visa (DNV)** provides the legal framework to live and work remotely in the country.

This ultimate guide covers everything you need to know about applying for the DNV in 2026, including the latest legislative changes that radically affect the application process.

## 1. Who Can Apply?

The Greece Digital Nomad Visa is strictly for **non-EU / non-EEA / non-Swiss citizens**. If you hold an EU passport, you enjoy freedom of movement and do not need a visa to relocate to Greece (though you still need to register locally for tax purposes).

You must be either:
- **An employee** working for a company registered outside of Greece.
- **A freelancer / self-employed individual** with clients based entirely outside of Greece.

## 2. The Income Requirement

The core requirement of the DNV is proving you have sufficient resources to support yourself without taking jobs away from the local Greek economy. 

The financial threshold is **€3,500 per month NET (after taxes)**. It is critical to understand that this is a *net* figure. Your gross salary must be high enough that your take-home pay is at least €3,500.

**Bringing Family:**
If you wish to bring your family, the minimum net income requirement increases:
- **+ 20%** for a dependent spouse or partner (€4,200 net total)
- **+ 15%** for each dependent child 

## 3. The 2026 Rule Change (Law 5275/2026)

> [!WARNING]
> **Critical Update:** Under Law 5275/2026, which took effect on February 5, 2026, the application process for the Digital Nomad Visa changed significantly. 

Previously, remote workers could enter Greece on a standard 90-day Schengen tourist visa and apply to "convert" their status to a Digital Nomad Visa from within the country. **This in-country conversion route has been completely abolished.**

In 2026, you **must** apply for a national Type-D Digital Nomad Visa at the Greek consulate or embassy in your home country (or country of legal residence) *before* traveling to Greece. Once you receive the Type-D visa, you can enter Greece and apply for the 2-year residence permit.

## 4. Required Documents

A successful application relies on a perfectly organized dossier. Missing a single stamp can lead to significant delays. The primary documents include:

- **Valid Passport:** Must be valid for at least six months beyond your intended stay.
- **Proof of Income:** Employment contracts, recent payslips, bank statements, or freelance invoices proving the €3,500 net monthly income.
- **Private Health Insurance:** This is mandatory. You must have a health insurance policy from a provider authorized to operate in Greece that covers you for the entire duration of your stay. Travel insurance is *not* accepted.
- **Criminal Record Certificate:** Issued by your home country. Crucially, this must bear an **Apostille stamp** and be officially translated into Greek.
- **Proof of Accommodation:** A rental agreement in Greece or a hotel booking for your initial stay.
- **Declaration Letter:** A formal declaration stating your intention to reside in Greece and confirming you will not provide services to Greek employers.

## 5. Validity, Timelines, and Fees

**Validity:**
The initial Type-D visa issued by the consulate is typically valid for 12 months. Upon entering Greece, you apply for the Digital Nomad Residence Permit, which is valid for **2 years** and is renewable.

**Processing Times:**
- **Visa Processing:** Approximately 10 working days at the consulate.
- **Permit Review:** Once in Greece, reviewing the permit application can take up to 40 working days. 
- **Total Timeline:** Expect the entire process, from gathering documents to holding your permit, to take anywhere from 1 to 3 months.

**State Fees:**
- Consular Visa Fee: **€75**
- Residence Permit Administrative Fee (e-Paravolo): **€1,016**

## 6. A Note on the 50% Tax Break (Article 5C)

Many digital nomads move to Greece drawn by rumors of a massive 50% income tax break. While real (under Article 5C of the Greek Tax Code), it is important to know that **holding a Digital Nomad Visa actually makes you ineligible for this tax break.**

To qualify for the 50% tax exemption, you must transition your tax residency to Greece and either be employed by a Greek legal entity or establish yourself as a registered freelancer with a Greek business entity. 

Because the Digital Nomad Visa strictly prohibits you from entering the Greek labor market or setting up a local business, you cannot legally meet the requirements for Article 5C while on a DNV. Simply working remotely on a DNV for your foreign employer does not trigger the tax break.
