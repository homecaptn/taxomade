Greece, Cyprus, Portugal and Spain all pitch themselves to remote workers. For a freelancer the real difference is not the weather. It is four things: the income tax scale, social security, whether a newcomer regime actually covers freelance income, and how VAT works. Here is how they compare in 2026.

## The short answer

- **Lowest income tax on a normal freelance profit:** Cyprus, with 0% up to €22,000 and a 35% top rate. Its newcomer exemptions, however, cover employees, not freelancers.
- **Social security:** Greece charges a fixed monthly amount (from €150.46 for new freelancers), which is cheap at mid and high profits and expensive at low ones. The other three charge a share of income, and Portugal largely exempts the first year.
- **Best newcomer regime for an ordinary freelancer:** Greece. Article 5C halves taxable freelance income for seven years. Portugal's IFICI and Spain's Beckham law exist, but most self-employed people do not qualify.
- **Lowest nomad visa threshold:** Spain, at about €2,849 a month.
- **Highest top tax rates:** Spain and Portugal, at 45–54% and 48% respectively.

## Income tax

**Greece** taxes income from 2026 at [9%, 20%, 26%, 34%, 39% and 44%](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5246/2025/arthro/3), with the top rate from €60,000. Freelancers get no tax-free allowance. On top come a 55% advance on next year's tax (halved in your first year) and, from year four, a minimum presumed income. Details in our [freelancer tax overview](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026).

**Cyprus** raised its tax-free band with the 2026 reform. Income up to [€22,000 is taxed at 0%](https://www.internationaltaxreview.com/article/2gq2poiivuype846e2yat/itrworldtax/cyprus-tax-reform-comprehensive-update-effective-from-1-january-2026), then 20%, 25%, 30% and 35% above €72,000.

**Portugal** runs nine brackets from 12.5% to 48% above €86,634 ([State Budget 2026](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/pt-pt/services/tax/documents/tax-alert-oe-2026.pdf)). Most freelancers use the simplified regime, where 75% of fees for listed professional services count as taxable income ([art. 31 CIRS](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/cirs_rep/Pages/irs31.aspx)), subject to a documented-expenses rule.

**Spain** splits income tax into a [state scale of 9.5% to 24.5%](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/en_gb/ayuda/manuales-videos-folletos/manuales-practicos/irpf-2025/c15-calculo-impuesto-determinacion-cuotas-integras/gravamen-base-liquidable-general/gravamen-estatal.html) plus a regional scale. Combined rates start around 19%, and the top rate ranges from about 45% to 54% depending on the region.

Income tax only, on **€50,000 of taxable income**, single, no special regime, no social security, no personal credits:

| Cyprus | €6,900 |
| Portugal | about €13,860 |
| Spain | depends on the region |

In Portugal, €50,000 of taxable income under the simplified regime corresponds to roughly €66,700 of fees. Social security changes the picture, which is the next section.

## Social security

**Greece** charges a fixed amount, whatever you earn. In 2026 the [lowest regular EFKA class](https://www.forin.gr/articles/article/88941/efka-egk-6-2026) is €250.77 a month, and new freelancers can apply for €150.46 for their first five years. Most freelancers add €10. That is about €1,925 to €3,130 a year, and contributions are deductible. More in [EFKA for freelancers](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece).

**Cyprus** charges self-employed people [16.6% for social insurance](https://cy.andersen.com/news/cyprus-news-cyprus-tax-reform-updates-effective-from-2026-2/) on insurable income, capped at €68,904 in 2026, plus [4% for GESY](https://rightax.com.cy/cyprus-gesy-contributions-guide/), the health system. The [basis is a minimum income set by occupation](https://www.businessincyprus.gov.cy/social-insurance-registration-and-contributions/), or your actual income on request.

**Portugal** charges [21.4% on 70% of service income](https://sisscontent.seg-social.pt/documents/10152/113014/folha_informativa.pdf/3190c94f-2c7a-4d12-b123-94a849917ef8), roughly 15% of fees, declared quarterly. Coverage normally starts in the twelfth month of activity, so the first year is largely contribution-free.

**Spain** links the monthly fee to net income. For 2026 the minimum fees run from [about €206 to €607 a month](https://upta.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/RES-ORDEN-COTIZACION-2026-RETA.pdf) across the bands. New autónomos can apply for a [reduced €80 rate](https://declarando.es/alta-como-autonomo/tarifa-plana-bonificaciones) (€88.64 including the equity surcharge) for 12 months, extendable if income stays below the minimum wage.

## Newcomer tax regimes

Most comparisons skip the key point: the headline regimes rarely fit a standard freelancer.

**Greece: Article 5C.** If you were not Greek tax resident in 5 of the previous 6 years and start a job or business in Greece, [50% of that income is exempt](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/5Γ) for seven years. Freelancing for foreign clients qualifies. EFKA is not reduced. Digital Nomad Visa holders are excluded. See [how the 50% break works](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c). Greece also has the [€100,000 non-dom regime](/guides/article-5c-vs-non-dom-regime) for foreign income, which can run in parallel with 5C.

**Cyprus: built for employees and investors.** The [50% exemption](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/Cyprus/Individual/Income-determination) (pay above €55,000, 17 years) and the 20% exemption (capped at €8,550, 7 years) apply to employment income. A [25% exemption](https://kpmg.com/cy/en/home/insights/2026/03/income-tax-exemption-for-repatriation-to-cyprus.html) that does cover business profits targets returning former residents. The non-dom status exempts dividends and interest from Special Defence Contribution for 17 years, which matters for investors more than for freelancers.

**Portugal: IFICI.** [Eligible income is taxed at 20% for ten years](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/apoio_contribuinte/Folhetos_informativos/Documents/incentivos_investigacao.pdf) if you were not resident in the previous five years. It covers self-employment only when the activity is on the list: research, teaching, highly qualified roles in qualifying companies, certified startups. A typical freelance designer or developer with foreign clients does not qualify.

**Spain: Beckham law.** [24% up to €600,000](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/en_gb/ayuda/manuales-videos-folletos/manuales-practicos/manual-tributacion-no-residentes/regimenes-opcionales/regimen-especial-impatriados.html) for the arrival year plus five, after five years of non-residence. Since 2023 it also covers remote employees, entrepreneurial activities and highly qualified startup professionals. Ordinary freelancing is not on the list.

## Digital nomad visas

All four offer a visa for non-EU remote workers. Thresholds in 2026:

- **Greece:** [€3,500 a month net](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5038/2023/arthro/68). No Greek clients or employer allowed, and therefore [no Article 5C](/guides/5c-vs-digital-nomad-visa).
- **Cyprus:** [€3,500 a month after tax](https://cy.andersen.com/news/the-digital-nomad-visa-scheme-in-cyprus/), with work for employers or clients abroad.
- **Portugal:** [four times the minimum wage](https://www.idealista.pt/news/node/54662), so €3,680 a month with the [2026 minimum wage of €920](https://apcmc.pt/legislacao/salario-minimo-nacional-para-2026-fixado-em-e-920/).
- **Spain:** [200% of the minimum wage](https://www.costaluzlawyers.com/es/visado-nomada-digital-espana-guia-completa-2026/), about €2,849 a month. Remote employees on this visa can apply for the Beckham law; self-employed holders mostly cannot.

EU citizens need none of this. They register as freelancers directly, which in Greece also opens the door to 5C. See [freelancer setup in Greece](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece).

## VAT

Standard rates are 24% in Greece, 19% in Cyprus, 23% in Portugal and 21% in Spain. Business clients in other EU countries are usually invoiced without local VAT in all four. Small-business exemptions differ: [€10,000 in Greece](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients), [€15,600 in Cyprus](https://www.mof.gov.cy/mof/tax/taxdep.nsf/All/88A7B920B681122AC22582510035432D), [€15,000 in Portugal](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/civa_rep/Pages/artigo-53-o-do-civa.aspx). Spain has [no general exemption](https://en.ara.cat/economy/brussels-takes-spain-to-european-court-for-not-exempting-smes-and-the-self-employed-from-declaring-vat_1_5674905.html), which the European Commission has challenged in court. Greece also adds strict reporting: every invoice goes to myDATA, and new businesses file VAT monthly for their first 24 months.

## Which one fits you?

- **Moderate profit, no regime:** Cyprus has the lowest income tax. Greece's fixed EFKA favours higher earners; Portugal's first-year exemption helps at the start.
- **Ordinary freelancer eligible for a newcomer regime:** Greece's 5C is the one that reliably covers freelance income.
- **Research, startup or highly qualified roles:** Portugal's IFICI and Spain's Beckham law are worth checking.
- **Non-EU, lower income:** Spain's visa threshold is the lowest; check the tax side separately.

Cost of living, healthcare and language matter as much as tax. For Greece, see our [cost of living guide](/guides/cost-of-living-greece-nomads).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Cyprus tax-free for freelancers?

No, but the first €22,000 of taxable income is taxed at 0% from 2026, and the top rate is 35%. Self-employed people pay 16.6% social insurance plus 4% for the health system (GESY). The 50% and 20% exemptions for newcomers apply to employment income, not to freelance profits.

### Can freelancers use Portugal's IFICI (NHR 2.0)?

Only if the freelance work itself is an eligible activity, such as research, teaching or a highly qualified role in a qualifying company or certified startup. Invoicing foreign clients as a self-employed professional does not qualify on its own. Where it applies, eligible income is taxed at 20% for 10 years.

### Can self-employed people use Spain's Beckham law?

Only in limited cases. Since 2023 the regime covers people who move to carry out an activity classed as entrepreneurial, and highly qualified professionals working for startups or in R&D. Ordinary freelancing is not among the qualifying reasons. Those who qualify pay 24% on income up to €600,000 for the year of arrival and five more years.

### Which digital nomad visa has the lowest income requirement: Greece, Cyprus, Portugal or Spain?

Spain, at 200% of the minimum wage, about €2,849 a month in 2026. Greece and Cyprus both ask for €3,500 a month net. Portugal asks for four times its minimum wage, €3,680 a month in 2026.

### Can digital nomad visa holders get Greece's 50% tax break?

No. Article 5C requires a job with a Greek employer or your own business activity in Greece, and the Digital Nomad Visa forbids both. DNV holders who become Greek tax residents are taxed under the normal rules.

*Rules as of October 2026. Tax rules in all four countries change often; check the current figures before you act.*
