Moving to Greece as a freelancer comes with two well-advertised benefits: the [50% income tax break under Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c/) and the reduced [EFKA starter class](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece/) for new freelancers. The cost that catches most people out is the advance tax: a prepayment of next year's income tax that lands on top of your first real tax bill. Here is how it works, followed by the smaller costs to budget for in your first years.

## The short answer

- **Advance tax (prepayment):** with each annual return you also prepay next year's tax: **55%** of the tax on your business income, **27.5%** on the first return with business income. From tax year 2027 (returns filed in 2028) the rate drops to **50%**.
- **It comes back:** the prepayment is credited against next year's bill, and any excess is refunded. But it has to be in the bank when the return is due.
- **Documents:** apostilles (typically up to about €50 each) and certified translations (roughly €15 to €30 per page) for the 5C application.
- **GEMI and chamber fees:** only for commercial activities.
- **Presumptive minimum income:** from year four, you can be taxed on a minimum profit based on the minimum wage, even if you declared less (reduced in years four and five).

## 1. The advance tax (prokatavoli forou)

This is the biggest shock for new freelancers in Greece, and a common reason for cash-flow problems in year two.

### How the prepayment works

When you file your annual tax return as a freelancer, you do not just pay the tax you owe for the previous year. **You also prepay part of the coming year's tax.** For business income the prepayment is **55%** of the income tax on that income ([Article 69 of the Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/69)). Tax that Greek clients already withheld from your invoices during the year is deducted from it. In the year you first earn business income, the prepayment is halved to 27.5%.

The following year, the prepayment is set off against your new tax bill. If it is higher than the tax actually due, the difference is refunded. You do not lose the money; you pay it about a year early.

### Worked example

Your final income tax for the year, after the 50% tax break, is €5,000, and no tax was withheld by clients.

| | First year with business income | Following years |
|---|---|---|
| Income tax for the year | €5,000 | €5,000 |
| Prepayment for next year | €1,375 (27.5%) | €2,750 (55%) |
| **Due with the return** | **€6,375** | **€7,750** |
| Minus last year's prepayment | €0 | −€1,375 or −€2,750 |

The jump happens in the second year: the full 55% replaces the halved rate, while only the smaller first-year prepayment is credited. With stable income, the cash flow evens out from year three.

### What changes with Law 5355/2026

[Law 5355/2026](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/5355/2026) (art. 12) cuts the prepayment for individuals with business income from 55% to 50% from **tax year 2027**, so the first return with the lower rate is the one filed in 2028. The return for tax year 2026, filed in 2027, still carries 55%, so the example above holds for it. The halving in the first year of business income stays.

If you trade through a company (IKE), the prepayment is 80% of the corporate tax, halved for the first three tax years ([Article 71](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/71)). Law 5355/2026 does not change the company rate.

### When you pay it

The prepayment is part of the same payment as your income tax: in full by the end of July, or in up to eight monthly instalments. Filing early and paying in full earns a discount (see the [tax deadlines calendar](/guides/greek-tax-deadlines-calendar-2026/)). The easiest way to absorb it is to put aside a share of every invoice from month one. The worked example in our [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026/) includes it.

## 2. Certified translations and apostilles

To apply for Article 5C, you prove that you were not a Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years, usually with tax residency certificates or equivalent evidence from your previous country. You cannot hand a [German](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide/), [British](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-uk-tax-guide/) or other foreign document to the Greek tax office as it is.

- **Apostille stamps:** unless exempt under a bilateral or EU rule, foreign public documents need an apostille. Fees are set by the issuing country, typically up to about €50 per document, plus courier fees.
- **Certified translations:** foreign documents submitted to the Greek state are translated into Greek by a translator from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' register of certified translators or by a Greek lawyer. Market rates are typically €15 to €30 per page, so budget €20 to €70 per document.

## 3. GEMI registration and chamber fees

Whether you register with GEMI (the General Commercial Registry) depends on your activity. Under Law 4919/2022, sole proprietors register if they carry out commercial acts as a profession, or supply goods or services with business risk through organised infrastructure or staff. A solo consultant, developer or designer working from a laptop is usually outside the obligation; a trader, e-shop or hospitality business is inside it. Accountants differ in practice, so settle this when you [choose your activity codes](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece/), not after the first chamber letter arrives.

If you do register, expect a registration fee and an annual GEMI fee, plus chamber fees where they apply; these vary by chamber.

## 4. The presumptive minimum income after year three

The cost many relocation guides skip. Since tax year 2023, Greek freelancers are taxed on at least a presumptive minimum income (τεκμαρτό εισόδημα), even if the declared profit is lower. The basis is the annual minimum wage: €12,320 for tax year 2025 (14 × €880) and about €12,880 for 2026 (14 × €920). Add-ons for staff costs and above-average turnover can raise it; from tax year 2026, Law 5355/2026 drops them for freelancers with a clean compliance record.

The phase-in for new businesses:

- **Years 1 to 3:** exempt.
- **Year 4:** reduced by two thirds.
- **Year 5:** reduced by one third.
- **From year 6:** applies in full.

For a part-time freelancer on the 5C break, this can matter more than any apostille fee. You can contest the amount with evidence or by requesting a tax audit. Details and the exceptions are in the [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026/).

## 5. AMKA and KEP visits

If you are a remote employee of a foreign company rather than a freelancer, you do not register a business. You need an AFM (tax number) and an AMKA (social security number). Whether you stay insured at home depends on your setup: a formal posting with an A1 certificate or genuine multi-state work keeps you in your home system; a full-time remote employee living in Greece normally falls under Greek EFKA, which the employer has to arrange. Details in our [EFKA guide](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece/).

An accountant can prepare and file your AFM application for you, but AMKA registration is not something accountants usually handle. AMKA is issued once you prove legal residence and becomes fully active once you also prove actual residence, for example with a lease; in practice this still usually means a visit to a KEP (Citizens' Service Centre) or EFKA office. The cost is time rather than money, and it helps to bring a Greek speaker.

## Plan for the friction

None of this should stop a move. The point is to budget for it: put the prepayment aside from month one, order your foreign tax residency certificates early, check whether GEMI applies to your activity before registering, and know when the presumptive income kicks in. The [5C calculator](/greece/5c-tax-calculator/) shows the net effect of the tax break.
