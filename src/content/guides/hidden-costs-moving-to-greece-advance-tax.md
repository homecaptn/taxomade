Moving to Greece as a freelancer comes with two well-advertised benefits: the [50% income tax break under Article 5C](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c) and the reduced [EFKA starter class of about €160 per month](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece) for new freelancers. It also comes with costs that rarely make it into the relocation pitch. Here they are, with the amounts to budget for in your first years.

## The short answer

- **Advance tax:** with your first annual return you pay 55% of your income tax again, as an advance on next year (27.5% in the year you first earn business income). Credited later, but it has to be in the bank.
- **Documents:** apostilles (roughly €15 to €100 each) and certified translations (roughly €15 to €30 per page) for the 5C application.
- **GEMI and chamber fees:** only for commercial activities; €30 per year if they apply.
- **Presumptive minimum income:** from year four, you are taxed on at least the minimum wage, whatever you declared.
- **AMKA:** no fee, but an in-person visit to a KEP or EFKA office.

## 1. The 55% advance tax (prokatavoli forou)

This is the biggest shock for new freelancers in Greece, and a common reason for cash-flow problems in year two.

When you file your annual tax return as a freelancer, you do not just pay the tax you owe for the previous year. **You also pay an advance on the coming year.** For business income the advance is **55%** of the income tax ([Article 69 of the Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/69)). In the year you first earn business income, it is halved to 27.5%.

**How the maths looks:** your final income tax bill for the year, after the 50% tax break, is €5,000. You pay that €5,000 plus €2,750 (55%) as an advance for next year: €7,750 in total. In your first year of business income the advance is €1,375.

The following year, the €2,750 is deducted from your new bill. You do not lose the money, but you need it on the account in July. The worked example in our [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026) includes it.

## 2. Certified translations and apostilles

To apply for Article 5C, you prove that you were not a Greek tax resident in 5 of the last 6 years, usually with tax residency certificates or equivalent evidence from your previous country. You cannot hand a [German](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-germany-tax-guide), [British](/guides/moving-to-greece-from-uk-tax-guide) or Spanish document to the Greek tax office as it is.

- **Apostille stamps:** unless exempt under a bilateral or EU rule, foreign public documents need an apostille. Depending on the country, that costs roughly €15 to €100 per document, plus courier fees.
- **Certified translations:** every foreign document submitted to the Greek state is translated into Greek by a translator from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' register of certified translators (searchable on gov.gr) or by a Greek lawyer. Legal minimum fees are €5 to €11 per page; market rates are typically €15 to €30 per page, so budget €20 to €70 per document.

## 3. GEMI registration and chamber fees

Whether you register with GEMI (the General Commercial Registry) depends on your activity. Under Law 4919/2022, sole proprietors register if they carry out commercial acts as a profession, or supply goods or services with business risk through organised infrastructure or staff. A solo consultant, developer or designer working from a laptop is usually outside the obligation; a trader, e-shop or hospitality business is inside it. Accountants differ in practice, so settle this when you [choose your activity codes](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece), not after the first chamber letter arrives.

If you do register: the one-off fee is small (tens of euros), the annual GEMI fee for a sole proprietorship is **€30**, and chamber membership on top is €10 to €20 per year and effectively voluntary.

## 4. The presumptive minimum income after year three

The cost most relocation guides skip entirely. Since 2024, Greek freelancers are taxed on at least a presumptive minimum income (τεκμαρτό εισόδημα) tied to the annual minimum wage, currently around €12,000 to €13,000, even if the declared profit is lower. New businesses are exempt for the first three years; in the fourth year the presumption is reduced by two thirds, in the fifth by one third, and from the sixth year it applies in full. For a part-time freelancer on the 5C break, this can matter more than any apostille fee. Details and the exceptions are in the [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026).

## 5. AMKA and KEP visits

If you are a remote employee of a foreign company rather than a freelancer, you do not register a business. You need an AFM (tax number) and an AMKA (social security number). Whether you stay insured at home depends on your setup: a formal posting with an A1 certificate or genuine multi-state work keeps you in your home system; a full-time remote employee living in Greece normally falls under Greek EFKA, which the employer has to arrange. Details in our [EFKA guide](/guides/efka-social-security-freelancers-greece).

An accountant can prepare and file your AFM application for you, but AMKA registration is not something accountants usually handle. AMKA is issued and then activated once you prove legal and actual residence; in practice this still usually means a visit to a KEP (Citizens' Service Centre) or EFKA office. The cost is time rather than money, and it helps to bring a Greek speaker.

## Plan for the friction

None of this should stop a move. The point is to budget for it: put the advance tax aside from month one, order your foreign tax residency certificates early, check whether GEMI applies to your activity before registering, and know when the presumptive income kicks in. The [5C calculator](/greece/5c-tax-calculator) shows the net effect of the tax break.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the advance tax (prokatavoli forou) for freelancers in Greece?

An advance of 55% of your income tax on business income, paid with your annual return towards next year, on top of the tax actually due. Halved to 27.5% in the year you first earn business income and credited against the following year's bill.

### Do I need an apostille and a certified translation for the Article 5C application?

Usually yes. Foreign tax residency certificates need an apostille (roughly €15 to €100 per document) and a certified Greek translation by a registered translator or a Greek lawyer, typically €15 to €30 per page.

### Do freelancers in Greece have to register with GEMI and pay chamber fees?

Only if the activity is commercial or uses staff or organised infrastructure. Most solo service professions do not. If you do, the annual GEMI fee is €30; chamber membership is €10 to €20 and effectively voluntary.

### What is the presumptive minimum income for freelancers in Greece?

From the fourth year of activity you are taxed on at least the annual minimum wage, around €12,000 to €13,000, whatever you declared. Years one to three are exempt, year four is reduced by two thirds and year five by one third.
