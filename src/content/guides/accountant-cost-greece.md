If you're a freelancer, remote worker or small business owner in Greece, an accountant isn't really optional — between myDATA e-invoicing, quarterly VAT declarations and annual returns, the Greek system is built around professional filing. The good news: accounting in Greece costs far less than most internationals expect. Here's what you'll actually pay in 2026 — and more importantly, what you get for it.

## The short answer

- **Traditional local accountant** (Greek only, in person, basic scope): €30–€60 per month — with each declaration often billed as a paid extra
- **Full-service digital accounting for internationals** (multilingual, all filings included): €100–€200 per month
- **Company bookkeeping (IKE, EE, OE):** €150–€350+ per month, depending on activity and payroll
- **Annual tax return only (individuals):** €50–€250 as a one-off

## The real comparison isn't the monthly number

Here's what most price lists won't tell you: the €40 offer and the €120 offer are usually not the same product at different prices — they're different products.

Most low-cost quotes cover basic ledger keeping only. Quarterly VAT declarations, myDATA transmission of your invoices, the annual tax return, responses to tax office notices — each billed separately, in Greek, when you remember to ask. Add it up over a year and you often land at the same total as the full-service rate, minus the response times, minus your language, and minus someone actively watching your deadlines.

So when comparing accountants, don't ask "how much per month?" — ask "what exactly is included?"

## What drives the price?

### 1. Your legal form

A sole trader (ατομική επιχείρηση) with simple books is the cheapest to serve. An IKE brings corporate filings and more complexity — expect roughly double.

### 2. Invoice volume

Five outgoing invoices a month is a different job than fifty. Most firms price in tiers.

### 3. Language and service level

Working in English, German, Spanish or Greek — with digital document handling, written confirmations and guaranteed response times — sits in the full-service segment. That's where internationals almost always end up, by choice.

### 4. Payroll

Each employee adds roughly €10–€25 per month.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need an accountant as a freelancer in Greece?

Legally you can file yourself — practically, almost nobody does. The myDATA system, Greek-only government portals and automatic fines for late or mismatched filings make professional handling the norm, even among Greeks.

### Why do internationals pay more than the €30–€60 local rate?

They don't pay more for the same thing — they buy a different scope: everything included, in their language, with someone reachable when it matters. Billed extras on a "cheap" plan typically close most of the gap anyway.

### Can I switch accountants mid-year?

Yes, anytime. The new accountant takes over your records and access codes; a proper handover takes about a week.

### Are accounting fees tax-deductible?

Yes — accounting costs are a business expense and reduce your taxable income.
