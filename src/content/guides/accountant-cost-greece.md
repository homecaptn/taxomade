If you're a freelancer, remote worker or small business owner in Greece, an accountant isn't really optional — between myDATA transmission of every invoice, [mandatory e-invoicing](/guides/e-invoicing-greece-explained) arriving for small businesses in November 2026, VAT returns and the annual return, the Greek system is built around professional filing. The good news: accounting in Greece costs far less than most internationals expect. Here's what you'll actually pay in 2026 — and more importantly, what you get for it.

## The short answer

- **Traditional local accountant** (Greek only, in person, basic scope): €30–€60 per month — with each declaration often billed as a paid extra
- **Full-service digital accounting for internationals** (multilingual, all filings included): €100–€200 per month
- **Company bookkeeping (IKE, EE, OE):** €150–€350+ per month, depending on activity and payroll — Greek price lists start partnerships (OE/EE) at around €120–€150 and IKEs with double-entry books at €180–€280
- **Annual tax return only (individuals):** €30–€250 as a one-off — a simple salaried return sits at the bottom of that range; foreign income, rental income or treaty questions push it to the top

## The real comparison isn't the monthly number

Here's what most price lists won't tell you: the €40 offer and the €120 offer are usually not the same product at different prices — they're different products.

Most low-cost quotes cover basic ledger keeping only. VAT returns (quarterly for most established freelancers with simplified books, but [monthly if you started your business on or after 1 January 2024](https://aade.gr/sites/default/files/2025-07/dt_03.07.2025.pdf) — with a quarterly option only after 24 months), myDATA transmission of your invoices, the annual tax return, responses to tax office notices — each billed separately, in Greek, when you remember to ask. Add it up over a year and you often land at the same total as the full-service rate, minus the response times, minus your language, and minus someone actively watching your deadlines.

So when comparing accountants, don't ask "how much per month?" — ask "what exactly is included?"

## What drives the price?

### 1. Your legal form

A sole trader (ατομική επιχείρηση) with simplified books is the cheapest to serve. An [IKE](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs) brings double-entry books, corporate filings and more complexity — expect at least double, often more: local price lists quote IKEs at €180–€280 per month against €40–€80 for a sole trader.

### 2. Invoice volume

Five outgoing invoices a month is a different job than fifty. Most firms price in tiers.

### 3. Language and service level

Working in English, German, Spanish or Greek — with digital document handling, written confirmations and guaranteed response times — sits in the full-service segment. That's where internationals almost always end up, by choice.

### 4. Payroll

Each employee adds roughly €15–€35 per month on Greek price lists, depending on sector and the number of staff.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need an accountant as a freelancer in Greece?

Legally you can file yourself — practically, almost nobody does. The myDATA system, Greek-only government portals and fixed fines for late filings — [€250 per late return on simplified books, €500 on double-entry books](https://www.taxheaven.gr/news/71451/prostima-diadikastikwn-parabasewn-kfd-dieykriniseis-kai-paradeigmata) under Article 54 of the Tax Procedure Code — make professional handling the norm, even among Greeks. See our [freelancer tax guide](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026) for what's involved.

### Why do internationals pay more than the €30–€60 local rate?

They don't pay more for the same thing — they buy a different scope: everything included, in their language, with someone reachable when it matters. Billed extras on a "cheap" plan typically close most of the gap anyway.

### Can I switch accountants mid-year?

Yes, anytime. The new accountant takes over your records and access codes; a proper handover takes about a week. We've written up the steps in our guide to [switching accountants in Greece](/guides/switching-accountants-greece).

### Are accounting fees tax-deductible?

For freelancers and companies, yes — accounting fees are a business expense under [Article 22 of the Greek Income Tax Code](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013/arthro/22) (Law 4172/2013) and reduce your taxable income, as long as they're invoiced and recorded in your books. The fee for a private individual's annual return is not deductible.
