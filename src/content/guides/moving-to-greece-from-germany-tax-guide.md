Moving from Germany to Greece is one of the most common cases we see: freelancers and remote workers, founders with a GmbH, and retirees. The Greek side is the same for everyone who moves here; what differs by country is the exit. This guide covers the German side and points you to the right Greek setup. Rules as of October 2026.

## The short answer

- **Deregister in Germany** within two weeks of moving out and keep the certificate.
- **The year you move** is still filed in Germany as one tax return; Greek income after the move raises your German rate but isn't taxed there.
- **Double tax treaty 1966:** freelance and employment income are taxed where you live and work, German rental income stays German, pensions are taxed in Greece.
- **Social security follows your work:** freelancers and employees working from Greece join EFKA, not the German system.
- **GmbH owners** face the German exit tax before they leave.
- **Retirees** keep their German pension and German health cover, taxed in Greece.
- **Article 5C** (50% off income tax for 7 years) is open to anyone moving from Germany who meets the conditions.

## 1. Before you leave: deregistration and loose ends

Under [§17 of the German registration law (BMG)](https://lexmea.de/en/gesetz/bmg/17), you deregister at your local registration office **within two weeks after moving out**, at the earliest one week before. It's free, and you receive an **Abmeldebescheinigung**. Keep it: banks, insurers and the broadcasting fee service will ask for it, and it is your first proof that you no longer live in Germany.

Also on the list:

- **Rundfunkbeitrag:** deregister actively with the Beitragsservice; it does not stop by itself.
- **Finanzamt:** tell your tax office your new address. If you ran a business, inform them that it has been relocated or closed.
- **Gewerbe:** if your activity was a registered trade, file a Gewerbeabmeldung ([§14 GewO](https://www.ihk.de/hamburg/produktmarken/beratung-service/gruendung/gruender/gewerbeanmeldung-1162420)). Liberal professions (Freiberufler) only inform the Finanzamt.
- **Church tax** ends with your German tax residency; no separate step is needed.
- **Your German flat:** if a home in Germany remains available to you, German tax law (§8 AO) can still treat you as resident there.

## 2. The year you move

German income tax is an annual tax. In the year you leave, you file **one German return** covering the months you lived there plus any German-source income afterwards ([§2 (7) EStG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/EStG/2.h.html)). Greek income earned after the move is not taxed in Germany, but it raises your German tax rate on the months before the move ([Progressionsvorbehalt, §32b EStG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/EStG/32b.html)).

From the following year, Germany only taxes German-source income, such as rent from a German property, as a non-resident. Non-residents get no basic allowance (Grundfreibetrag). If at least 90% of your income remains taxable in Germany, or your non-German income stays below the basic allowance, you can apply to be treated as a resident taxpayer ([§1 (3) EStG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/EStG/1.html)).

## 3. The double tax treaty

Germany and Greece use the [treaty of 1966](https://rechtsanwalt-griechenland.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Doppelbesteuerungsabkommen_Deutschland_Griechenland.pdf); no newer treaty is in force, although the BEPS multilateral instrument has modified some provisions since 2025. What it means in practice:

| Income | Taxed in |
|---|---|
| Freelance / self-employed work done from Greece | Greece, unless you keep a fixed base in Germany |
| Employment | Where the work is physically performed; days worked in Germany stay German |
| Rent from German property | Germany (Greece gives credit) |
| German statutory and private pensions | Greece only |
| German civil service pensions (Beamte) | Germany |
| German dividends | Germany may keep up to 25% (the excess over the German 26.375% withholding can be reclaimed); Greece credits |

If both countries see you as resident, the treaty's tie-breaker decides: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode. Germany exempts Greek income with progression; Greece credits German tax.

## 4. Freelancers and remote workers

As a freelancer, you register a sole proprietorship in Greece, join EFKA and invoice from your Greek business. Your German clients are still your clients:

- **Invoices to German businesses** are issued without VAT under the reverse charge, with both VAT numbers; your Greek number has to be registered in VIES. See [VAT for freelancers with foreign clients](/guides/vat-freelancers-greece-foreign-clients).
- **The German Kleinunternehmerregelung** no longer applies to you as a German-established business. For business clients it doesn't matter anyway, because the reverse charge applies.
- **Social security:** under [EU Regulation 883/2004](https://www.ihk.de/blueprint/servlet/resource/blob/4699626/f8b52df710e52ed806f2565e825f693b/ihk-merkblatt-zur-a1-bescheinigung-data.pdf), you are insured where you work, so with EFKA. An A1 certificate from Germany only covers temporary postings of up to 24 months, not a permanent move.
- **Article 5C:** if you were not Greek tax resident in five of the last six years, 50% of your Greek business income can be exempt from income tax for seven years. See [how the 50% tax break works](/guides/50-percent-tax-break-article-5c).

What the Greek setup looks like: [setting up as a freelancer in Greece](/guides/freelancer-setup-greece) and [freelancer taxes in Greece 2026](/guides/freelancer-taxes-greece-2026).

## 5. Employees of a German employer

If you keep your German employment contract and work from Greece, two things change:

- **Tax:** income for work physically performed in Greece is taxable in Greece. Days worked in Germany remain German.
- **Social security:** working permanently from Greece puts you under Greek social security. The [EU framework agreement on cross-border telework](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland), which lets some remote employees stay in their employer's system, has been signed by Germany but **not by Greece**. Your employer would need to register for Greek contributions, which many German employers decline.

This is why most German remote employees who move here switch to the self-employed route and invoice their former employer. Article 5C as an employee requires a Greek employer or a Greek branch of the German company. Details and risks in [5C for EU remote workers](/guides/5c-for-eu-remote-workers).

## 6. GmbH owners and founders

Two German rules matter before you move:

**Exit tax (§6 AStG).** If you hold **at least 1%** of a corporation (GmbH, UG, AG) and were taxable in Germany for **7 of the last 12 years**, Germany taxes the gain on your shares when you leave, as if you had sold them ([EY overview](https://www.ey.com/de_de/technical/news-zum-internationalen-mitarbeitereinsatz/auslandseinsatz-und-wegzugsbesteuerung)). Until 2021, moves within the EU got an indefinite interest-free deferral; for departures since 2022 the tax is due. On application, it can be paid in **seven annual instalments**, interest-free but usually against security. If you return within seven years (extendable to twelve), the tax falls away, provided you didn't distribute more than 25% of the share value in the meantime. Every year by 31 July, you also have to confirm your address and your shareholding to the Finanzamt, electronically since 2025.

**Place of management (§10 AO).** If the sole managing director runs the GmbH from Greece, its place of effective management may move with them. The company can then become tax resident in Greece under the treaty. Talk to a German Steuerberater before the Abmeldung, not after.

For a new company in Greece, see [setting up an IKE in Greece](/guides/ike-greece-setup-annual-costs).

## 7. Retirees

- **Your pension follows you.** The Deutsche Rentenversicherung pays to a Greek account; [once a year you confirm you are alive](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Ueber-uns-und-Presse/Presse/Meldungen/2026/260602-rente-im-ausland) (Lebensnachweis).
- **Tax:** statutory and private pensions are taxed in Greece only; German civil service pensions stay in Germany. The [Finanzamt Neubrandenburg](https://www.vlh.de/krankheit-vorsorge/altersbezuege/diese-steuerregeln-gelten-auch-fuer-rentner-im-ausland.html) is responsible for pensioners abroad and may still request a return.
- **Health insurance:** if you receive only a German pension and were compulsorily insured in Germany, you stay in the German pensioners' scheme after an EU move ([EU rules](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/when-living-abroad/health-insurance-cover/faq/index_en.htm)). Ask your Krankenkasse for the **S1 form**; it registers you with the Greek system (EOPYY), while Germany bears the cost.
- **Greek side:** you file an annual Greek tax return. Greece also offers a flat-tax regime for foreign pensioners who move their residency here (Article 5B); whether it beats normal taxation depends on your income.

## 8. Health insurance for working-age movers

Your German statutory insurance (GKV) ends when you gain cover in Greece through work, which is the case once you register with EFKA ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/gesundheit-pflege/krankenversicherung/krankenversicherung-was-gilt-bei-langzeitaufenthalten-im-ausland-63338)). The European Health Insurance Card is for temporary stays only. A private German policy (PKV) continues within the EU. If you don't rule out returning, ask your private insurer about an Anwartschaftsversicherung, which preserves your right to re-enter the policy without a new health check.

## 9. Car and household goods

Within the EU there are no customs formalities for household goods. For your car, Greece offers [relief from registration tax on transfer of residence](https://aade.gr/en/greeks-abroad-non-residents/customs-exemptions-relating-transfer-residence-greece/customs-and-tax-exemptions-individuals-who), if you lived abroad for at least two years, used the car for at least six months, and don't sell it for a year. The certificate comes from the Greek consulate in Germany; get it before you move.

## 10. Returning Greeks

If you are Greek and lived in Germany, everything above applies to you as well. Article 5C does not depend on nationality: if you were not Greek tax resident in five of the last six years, you can apply.

### Is my German pension taxed in Greece or in Germany?

Civil service pensions (Beamtenpensionen) remain taxable in Germany. The German tax office for pensioners abroad (Finanzamt Neubrandenburg) may still ask for a return.
